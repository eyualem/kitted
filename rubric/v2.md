# Rubric v2: the style rules, re-checked against 2025–26 sources

> **Superseded by [v3.md](v3.md) (Oct 6, 2026).** Kept as a record. The rule research and evidence below still apply; v3 changes how the score is built.

*Oct 6, 2026. Every rule was re-researched using only sources from Jan 2025 or later (about 70 dated sources, including Real Men Real Style, FashionBeans, Highsnobiety, Complex, Ape to Gentleman, The Modest Man and Permanent Style, plus dated videos from Alex Costa, Tim Dessaint and Alpha M). Full evidence with links and quotes is in `v2-evidence/fit.md`, `v2-evidence/color.md` and `v2-evidence/proportion_occasion.md`. The frozen code is `extraction-test/rubrics/rubric_v2.py`.*

## How a score works
- An outfit with **no problems = 9.0.** A 10 is rare.
- Each problem takes points off (lowest possible score: 3.0).
- **Rules depend on your chosen styles.** What's wrong for smart casual can be right for streetwear.
- **Never judged:** face, body, weight, skin tone. Traditional and cultural clothing = "not rated."
- **Nothing outside the photo costs points.** If shoes or the bottom half aren't visible, their rules are skipped (already in the code), and the card says "Partial check: shoes not included." In v3 their credits also come off the maximum, so a partial check can still reach 10. App flow: `demo-app-spec.md` §5.6.
- Costs below are approximate points off the 10-point score.

## What changed from v1
1. **Neutral colors no longer count as "too many colors."** Classic outfits like navy jacket + light-blue shirt + grey trousers + brown shoes were losing points. Now only *accent* colors count.
2. **Olive is a neutral.** Burgundy and sage are "muted": never loud, and count as half an accent.
3. **Mixing patterns is allowed** when they differ in scale or type (fine stripes with a big check, graphic tee with flannel).
4. **Sneakers:** running shoes, retro runners and chunky sneakers with jeans are **fine**. Only bulky shoes with skinny or slim pants, or sneakers with a suit, lose points.
5. **Distressed-by-design clothes** (raw denim fades, ripped jeans, pre-worn sneakers) are no longer counted as "worn out."
6. **"Oversized" isn't a mistake by itself anymore.** It only loses points with a visible too-big sign (sleeves covering hands, hem past the crotch).
7. **New rules:** sleeves covering hands, visible fabric strain, untucked dress shirt at work, shirt hanging below a blazer, and everything-oversized (outside streetwear).
8. **Wide-leg pants resting on the shoe aren't "pooling."**
9. **The beginner default fit is now "straight / relaxed-straight,"** the 2026 middle ground (5 sources), not "slim-straight."
10. **Two new styles: preppy and gorpcore** (the outdoor look). Work, interview and wedding are each **split** into casual and formal versions.
11. **A code bug is fixed:** Python's rounding sometimes skipped penalties for blended styles.

## The rules

### Fit and care
| Rule | What it means | Cost | Exceptions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pants pooling | Fabric collapses or drags heavily on the shoe | −0.8 | Streetwear with relaxed/wide pants: none. Streetwear with slim pants: −0.4. A wide leg *resting* on the shoe is fine |
| Full break on narrow pants | Slim/skinny pants bunching at the shoe | −0.2 | Only when clearly visible |
| Shirt too long | Untucked tee/shirt hangs past the crotch | −0.8 | Streetwear; intentionally oversized tops |
| Too big (visible sign) | Oversized top **and** sleeves covering hands or hem past the crotch | −0.6 | Streetwear |
| Sleeves covering hands | Shirt, jacket or knit sleeves over the hands | −0.4 | Streetwear hoodies: −0.2 |
| Visible strain | Buttons gaping, X-shaped creases, tight thighs (the fabric, never the body) | −0.6 | — |
| Shirt hanging below a blazer | Inner shirt hem shows below a tailored jacket | −0.6 | Casual jackets (denim, bomber, overshirt) are fine |
| Stains | Clearly visible stain | −1.2 | Only if the AI is sure |
| Wrinkles | Visibly wrinkled | −0.4 | — |
| Worn out | Pilling, lint, stretched neckline, dirty shoes | −0.4 | **Not** for distressed-by-design items. Scuffed sneakers in streetwear/workwear/vintage: −0.2 |
| Care total | — | max −1.6 | — |

### Tucking
| Rule | What it means | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Untucked dress shirt | A dress shirt left out with a blazer, suit or trousers, or at work / interviews / events | −0.6 |

Casual shirts untucked: fine. **Half tuck:** never penalized and never suggested (sources disagree). **Full tuck** is the fix for wide, pleated or high-rise trousers.

### Proportion
| Rule | What it means | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Top looseness vs. your style | Looser or tighter than your styles expect | −0.45 per step off |
| Pant shape vs. your style | Skinnier or wider than your styles expect | −0.45 per step off |
| Big top + skinny pants | Classic mismatch | −0.3 |
| Everything oversized | Huge top **and** wide pants, outside streetwear and gorpcore | −0.3 |
| Bulky shoes + narrow pants | Chunky sneakers or running shoes with skinny/slim pants | −0.45 |
| Rule of thirds | — | Tip only (front tuck or cropped layer), never a penalty |

### Color and patterns
| Rule | What it means | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Too many accent colors | 3+ non-neutral colors (muted ones count half). **All-neutral outfits are never penalized** | −0.9 |
| Too many colors overall | 5+ color families **and** 2+ accents | −0.3 |
| Bright clash | 2+ bright colors on main pieces (shoes count half) | −0.6 (−0.3 for "bold" users or streetwear) |
| Same pattern, same size | Two of the same pattern at the same scale (two mid plaids) | −0.6 |
| 3+ patterns | Outside streetwear | −0.6 |
| Too many focal points | More standout pieces (bright color, big pattern, big logo) than your boldness allows: subtle 1, a little 1.5, bold 2.5 (+1 for streetwear) | −0.3 per extra (max −0.6) |
| Tonal / all-black | Never penalized | Tip: vary texture or shade |
| Belt matching shoes | Only work, interview and event outfits with dress shoes | Tip only |

**Neutrals:** white, cream, greys, charcoal, black, navy, light blue, khaki/beige, tan/camel, brown, all denim, **olive**.
**Muted (half an accent, never loud):** burgundy, sage.
**Always bright:** red, orange, yellow, pink, purple. Green and blue count as bright only if vivid (kelly green, cobalt); forest and dusty blue don't.

### Occasion
| Rule | What it means | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Too casual | Below the occasion's dressiness | −1.5 per level (max −3) |
| Way too formal | 2+ levels above (slightly overdressed is fine) | −0.6 |
| Sneakers too casual for the outfit | Shoes 3+ levels below the dressiest piece (suit + running shoes) | −1.8 (white leather sneakers exempt) |
| Big logos | Large visible logos | −0.9 (−1.35 at work or interviews). Exempt: streetwear, vintage sport |

## Style bands
| Style | Top looseness (1 fitted → 5 very oversized) | Pants |
|---|---|---|
| **No style picked** | 2–3.5 | slim → relaxed (centered on straight) |
| Smart casual | 2–3 | slim → relaxed |
| Minimalist (incl. Scandinavian) | 2–4 | straight → relaxed |
| Old money / quiet luxury | 2–3 | straight → relaxed |
| Workwear (incl. Japanese Americana) | 3–4 | straight → relaxed |
| Vintage sport (incl. athleisure) | 3–4 | straight → relaxed |
| Streetwear (incl. skater) | 3–5 | straight → wide |
| **Preppy (new)** | 2–3 | slim → straight |
| **Gorpcore / outdoor (new, optional)** | 3–4 | straight → wide |

Flares and bootcut count as "straight"; barrel legs count as "wide."

## Occasions
| Occasion | Target dressiness |
|---|---|
| Everyday / Class | 2–3 |
| Date / Going out | 2.5–3.5 |
| Work (casual / tech / startup) | 2.5–3.5 |
| Work (business casual) | 3–4 |
| Interview (type unknown) | 3.5–4.5 |
| Interview (tech / creative) | 3–4 |
| Interview (corporate) | 4–5 |
| Wedding (casual) | 3–4 |
| Wedding (formal / cocktail) / Event | 4–5 (generic event 3.5–4.5) |

The app asks "what kind?" for work, interviews and weddings when it matters.

## Advice only (doesn't change the score)
Shoulder seam position, half tuck, belt-and-shoe match (dressy occasions only), the rule of thirds, tonal outfits (vary texture), and a "next level" idea (add one point of interest) when an outfit is already clean.
