# Jaye Alexander — transcript notes (batch B: wardrobes, jackets, aesthetics, accessories)
Videos read: 7 (Jz9QVWgBbOg, Pxeeh-caTJg, RIuzSb8OLMM, ws2jf0Yp_4c, Ulx1vjAREu0, 5Dfr0PXTTpM, ctdMGExbNws).

**How he judges an outfit (reader's summary).** Jaye ("JA") judges by whether an outfit is *basic* or *intentional*. His base is plain and neutral (black, blue, white; often all black), and he says himself that his fits are basic "from a surface level". What lifts a fit, in his order: (1) **fit and proportion of the clothes** (tailored, not boxy unless meant to be; the right rise; flare or straight legs used on purpose; layer lengths that work together, e.g. a cropped tee, not a long one, under a leather jacket); (2) **purposeful layering** (a clean, close base, at most 2–3 layers, each adding colour, pattern or material contrast, the outer layer setting the silhouette); (3) **one statement piece or accent** (a chain, a loud shoe, a bright scarf on an all-black fit) that turns a basic fit "not basic"; (4) **accessories as the finish** ("icing on the cake", "seasoning"), with balance: never louder than the clothes, and 1–2 details beat 4–6. He punishes randomness above all: pieces stacked without purpose, a bulky or sloppy base, head-to-toe designer, accessories that drown the clothes, or loud pieces at the wrong occasion. He is permissive about cuts (skinny, baggy, flare all fine), mixes styles on purpose (his favourite, "business core", puts a dress shirt and tie with jeans and a leather jacket) and leans hard on confidence and "your own style". Wardrobe videos are mostly shopping lists; the judging content sits in the jacket guide's proportion and layering sections, the spring layering section, the "perfect fit" breakdown in the wardrobe video, and the accessorizing video. Body, physique, hair, skin and hygiene content was skipped as the brief asks (it is frequent: "being in shape is style"). Auto-caption quality is poor in places; ws2jf0Yp_4c reads like a machine-translated dub (stilted, some words clearly wrong), so its quotes are less reliable.
