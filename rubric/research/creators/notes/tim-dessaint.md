Videos read: 5 (-V2bE8nDsdo, SNjfZ5NKndc, f1Ub9Zku0lI, poTk87gGHtg, wIib9hzVUuw).

Reader's summary: Tim judges an outfit first by **proportion and silhouette**: how the volume of the top relates to the bottom, whether trousers have room in the thigh and land on the shoe with one clean break, and whether the shoes carry the line of the trouser. Fit, to him, is not one correct size per item; it is a range of cuts (slim, straight, wide, boxy, oversized, drop shoulder) that you combine on purpose. A bad outfit is an extreme mismatch (oversized top over skinny jeans), too tight (pulling, creases, bunching), or busy (color-wheel pops, multicolor scarf, novelty details). An okay outfit fits but is "basic and predictable" (all one color family, stock pieces). A good one is clean and balanced with neutrals anchoring it and small color ties (the cap echoes the jeans). A great one balances **smart and casual** (tee tucked into tailored trousers, a tank under a blazer, sweatpants with minimal loafers), builds around one statement piece or a subtle "3%" design detail, adds texture contrast and muted, deep colors, and looks effortless, "without trying too hard". He openly rejects color-wheel theory and the idea of "timeless" pieces, and says any style is valid if it is yours. Two of the five videos (signature style, timeless) are mostly wardrobe and mindset, with few outfit-level rules.
