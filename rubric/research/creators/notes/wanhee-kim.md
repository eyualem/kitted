# Wanhee Kim — transcript notes
Videos read: 7 (1wUp3RSAEqI, 52A8D55bqL8, Mr-GzwGQ1qY, dKl6pohkzoA, e2MgmGDLV9Q, rNCrnP-os0U, tNVdA_arEa8).

**How he judges an outfit.** Wanhee judges by restraint first. The bad outfit is the one that "shouts": too many cool pieces, loud colors all over, big logos or graphics, brand-stacking, tight muscle-fit tops and skinny jeans, stiff brand-new clothes. The safe base is simple basics in a small neutral palette (white, black, navy, grey, beige, brown, khaki, military green), straight or regular fits, three colors at most (black and white count as half). On top of that base, an outfit gets good through details done right: the right trouser length, tone-in-tone color, dark around a bright piece, a relaxed (not tight, not huge) fit, and exactly **one** "point" (포인트, an accent: white socks with all black, a ring, a texture, one color). It gets great through things that make people look twice without knowing why: "character" or "mystery" in an ordinary piece (a wash, a perfect white tee), clothes that look worn-in and lived in, and a quiet "witty" twist (sneakers with tailoring, a print shirt under a black blazer), all looking almost unintentional. He says over and over that the person (movement, personality, passion) matters more than the clothes; that part is not rateable and is noted only as mindset. He is humble ("I'm not even that well dressed") and frames most rules as beginner-safe defaults, with oversized/boxy styles as "another territory" he personally avoids.

Caption notes: Korean terms are often mangled by auto-captions. My best reading of each is given with "(caption unclear)".
