## Why you look bad in all black clothes [Vn6KlXkU2xA] (2024-01-31) · Frugal Aesthetic

- **Depth inside a narrow palette:** in one color, get variety from value (faded vs new black, Vn6 [02:39]) and texture (leather, nylon, denim, Vn6 [05:56]–[07:02]), not from more hues.
- **Pick colors from a personal palette, not the wheel** (TQi2 [03:41], [07:57], [11:05]); avoid matchy-matchy (TQi2 [04:11], [10:04]).

**Strongest interactions (the frames worth shooting)**
1. **One color amplifies the silhouette** (Vn6 [09:16]–[09:46]): the same proportions read more strongly in all black. A bad shape costs more; a deliberate extreme can work.
2. **Shape and texture stand in for color contrast** (Vn6 [10:19], [05:56], [06:28]): fitted top over relaxed bottom "creates good contrast"; leather boots peeking under baggy black pants add contrast "even though they're both black".
3. **The shoe as the color source** (TQi2 [10:34]–[11:05]): matching the jacket to the shoe works but caps the outfit; the same shoe with palette colors that each have a role works better.
4. **Covering a fit fault** (WMwH [05:30]–[06:05]): a bomber or a loud shoe pulls the eye off a bad fit but doesn't fix it.
5. **Monochrome on a bold color vs matched by formula** (TQi2 [10:04]).
