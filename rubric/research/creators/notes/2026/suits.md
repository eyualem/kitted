# Suits and tailoring (outsiders): transcript notes (T95)

Videos read: z_acMbzNfoQ (2025-03-04, The Armoury / Mark Cho, The Armoury's Guide to How A Suit Should Fit) · 4fb4GyOylK8 (2025-01-22, GQ / Ian Meiers, A Master Tailor's Guide To The Perfect Suit) · bLcGL6rKBBg (2025-09-23, GQ / Ian Meiers, Master Tailor Reveals The Most Common Suit Mistakes) · pQctf3wAlpI (2024-10-12, Justin Kwan, Jacket Fit: Myth Busting Slim Fit + 6 Point Fit Check) · skd8U8dmIP4 (2025-08-02, Justin Kwan, 3 Secrets to A Perfect Fit: It's in the Proportions).

**Why this batch exists.** The four (Tim, Wanhee, Jaye, Frugal) barely cover suits, so these five outsiders fill the gap on purpose. Each outsider rule carries an `agrees with the four:` line. Where an outsider clashes with the four, **the four win**: the rule is marked `→ dropped` and kept only so the clash is on record. The test of the four was run against `creators-rules.md`, the October notes in `notes/*.md`, and a keyword sweep of every kept transcript of the four in `inputs/youtube/clean/` (blazer, suit, tailor, trouser, break, rise, taper, sleeve, shoulder, pleat, belt, overdress), plus a full read of 7K2qRZaXX3w (Jaye, overdressing) and -V2bE8nDsdo (Tim, blazers). What the four themselves say about suits is gathered in its own section near the end, with timestamps.

**Ignored as the brief says:** Kwan's head-to-shoulder ratio, "look taller", arm length and model-agency talk (skd8U8dmIP4 [02:42]–[03:50]); every "dropped shoulder", "round back", "prominent chest", belly and posture cause in the GQ mistakes video (only the clothing sign is kept: the crease, the gap, the bow); cloth origin by country, super numbers, lining fibers, horn sources, ready-to-wear vs bespoke cost, Kwan's tailoring plugs.

**What a photo can show.** The app sees one front photo. Many outsider faults show only from the back (back creases, collar roll, the back of the trousers) or need a tailor's eye (armhole height, sleeve pitch, balance). Each rule says so in `holds when`.

## The Armoury's Guide to How A Suit Should Fit [z_acMbzNfoQ] (2025-03-04) · The Armoury (Mark Cho)

### Rules
- **R1. The jacket's shoulder sits a little past the natural shoulder, for breadth across the top.** · part: tailoring · strength: lift
  - holds when / doesn't: a little, not "hanging off" (see R9); a size too small reads narrow in the shoulder. Front-photo visible only as the shoulder line vs the sleeve head; not steady.
  - interacts with: chest (R2) and waist (R3): a slightly wider shoulder is what makes the waist look shaped without taking it in.
  - source: The Armoury · z_acMbzNfoQ · [01:08] · "my jacket extends a little bit further"
  - on screen: yes (he shows himself without, then with, the jacket)
  - others: Kwan (pQctf3wAlpI [03:17], skd8U8dmIP4 [03:50]) says the same.
  - agrees with the four: yes (Tim, wIib9hzVUuw, [03:02]–[03:33], a coat shoulder "slightly wider with enough structure"; Tim, -e9Is50-fn0, [08:46], broad structured shoulders "even if you're dressed in a casual outfit"; Tim, 0ilmLux6mPg, [07:42], a jacket "structured with broad shoulders")
- **R2. The chest has drape: a little room between the jacket and the body, never pulled flat.** · part: fit · strength: lift
  - holds when / doesn't: "a little bit of drape but not too much" [05:59]. Drape itself is mostly invisible in a photo; its absence shows as pulling (R33, R40).
  - interacts with: shoulder (R1) and waist (R3).
  - source: The Armoury · z_acMbzNfoQ · [01:40] · "the chest has what we call drape"
  - on screen: yes (jacket on)
  - others: Kwan (pQctf3wAlpI [03:17]) "drapes naturally"; GQ (bLcGL6rKBBg [02:38]) "hug the body without being too tight or too loose".
  - agrees with the four: yes (Tim, -V2bE8nDsdo, [01:31], "a slightly relaxed fit, which I prefer in a blazer"; all four's "nothing tight", creators-rules A2)
- **R3. Shape the waist by giving the chest and shoulders a bit more, not by taking the waist in until it's tight.** · part: silhouette · strength: nuance
  - holds when / doesn't: the outsider's own preference; he says taking it in "uncomfortably tight" is "very obvious and visible".
  - interacts with: R1, R2; against slim-fit tightness (R36).
  - source: The Armoury · z_acMbzNfoQ · [02:11] · "give you the illusion of a waist"
  - on screen: yes
  - others: Kwan (pQctf3wAlpI [02:13]) same mechanism ("hourglass shape and cut").
  - agrees with the four: yes (Tim, -V2bE8nDsdo, [01:31], relaxed blazer; Jaye, Jz9QVWgBbOg, [13:11], "skin tight jackets" ruin the fit)
- **R4. Suit trousers sit high: at or just below the navel, waistband above the hip bone. "These are not like jeans."** · part: proportion · strength: lift
  - holds when / doesn't: trousers with a jacket; jeans are allowed lower (GQ, R30). Visible only when the jacket is open or off, or the shirt is tucked.
  - interacts with: the jacket (a high rise under a jacket keeps the leg line long, R44); the tuck (a high rise leaves room to tuck).
  - source: The Armoury · z_acMbzNfoQ · [02:45] · "trousers typically should be sitting on the belly button"
  - on screen: yes (he points to the waistband)
  - others: GQ (bLcGL6rKBBg [09:17]) at the hip bone; Kwan (skd8U8dmIP4 [02:12]) "higher on the waist".
  - agrees with the four: yes (Jaye, RIuzSb8OLMM, [13:43]–[14:14], low rise "cuts your proportions", high rise "elongates your legs"; Wanhee, RtxJqd5Drps, [01:40], with a blazer trousers "worn high", "a lot of formal wear will be like this"; Frugal, XtcY-zmoS-E, [03:06], wide high-waisted trousers his most worn). Nuance: Jaye calls mid rise "the safest" for everyday pants.
- **R5. Not too narrow a leg: a narrow trouser catches on the leg and the line wobbles; a fuller leg falls straight and crisp.** · part: silhouette · strength: fault (when narrow) / lift (straight, crisp)
  - holds when / doesn't: suit and dress trousers; he wants "a nice long straight crisp line".
  - interacts with: the hem (R6, R7): weight at the bottom keeps the line straight.
  - source: The Armoury · z_acMbzNfoQ · [02:45] · "I don't like having overly narrow legs"
  - on screen: yes (his own trousers)
  - others: Kwan (skd8U8dmIP4 [01:05]) calls 2000s slim-fit-everything a "dark era".
  - agrees with the four: yes (Tim, wIib9hzVUuw, [03:33], "a precise taper rather than being slim all over"; Tim, wIib9hzVUuw, [10:40], "Enough room in the thighs, straight cut"; Tim, vQ6_okzfNSs, [04:16], suits "no longer" need "a slim fit cut"; Wanhee, rNCrnP-os0U, [02:34], not super skinny)
- **R6. No break: the trouser just touches the shoe, the front resting on its top.** · part: proportion · strength: lift
  - holds when / doesn't: he says it's "a preference thing". Touching the shoe is the shared core with Tim; the outsiders differ on no break vs one break (GQ prefers one, R46).
  - interacts with: the cuff (R7); the leg width (R5).
  - source: The Armoury · z_acMbzNfoQ · [03:17] · "the trouser is just barely touching the shoe"
  - on screen: yes ("as you can see")
  - others: GQ (bLcGL6rKBBg [10:52]) single break as the classic.
  - agrees with the four: yes (Tim, SNjfZ5NKndc, [00:00]–[00:30], "should touch the shoe and potentially have one clean break"; Tim, 0ilmLux6mPg, [08:15], pleated trousers "barely breaking on the shoes"; Tim, wIib9hzVUuw, [08:07], trousers meeting the shoe seamlessly)
- **R7. A turn-up (4 cm) gives the hem weight, so the trouser hangs straight instead of flapping.** · part: tailoring · strength: nuance
  - holds when / doesn't: "ideally", and a preference. GQ prefers no turn-ups because they read more casual (R28): the outsiders disagree with each other.
  - interacts with: R5, R6.
  - source: The Armoury · z_acMbzNfoQ · [03:17] · "gives the trous a little bit extra weight at the bottom" (caption "trous")
  - on screen: yes
  - others: clashes with GQ (4fb4GyOylK8 [15:21]).
  - agrees with the four: not covered by the four (Tim's cuff complaint, wIib9hzVUuw [08:07], is about cuffed jeans over a contrasting sneaker, not a trouser turn-up). Not a scoring rule: the outsiders split and the photo can't tell a turn-up reliably.
- **R8. Start from the size closest to the goal; alterations can only do so much.** · part: fit · strength: nuance
  - holds when / doesn't: buying advice (for Kit), not a photo rule.
  - interacts with: GQ R34 (buy on the chest, tailor the waist).
  - source: The Armoury · z_acMbzNfoQ · [04:19] · "you don't want to start with something that's really far off"
  - on screen: yes (three sizes prepared)
  - others: GQ (bLcGL6rKBBg [03:10]) same idea.
  - agrees with the four: yes (Jaye, CI-wkEOHRAg, [00:31], "just go get a tailor"; Frugal, fit-first and hemming, XtcY-zmoS-E [05:38]; Tim, zJ5LCenTcf8, [01:10]–[01:40], sarcastic "never tailor your clothes", meaning do)
- **R9. A size too small is narrow in the shoulder and tight in the armhole, and the buttoned front pulls; a size too big has too much chest and too much waist, so the shape is lost. Some like a relaxed fit, and that's allowed.** · part: fit · strength: fault (too small) / nuance (too big)
  - holds when / doesn't: he says the too-big size "you can always get away with" if you like a really relaxed fit. Only the too-small half is a clear fault.
  - interacts with: R1–R3.
  - source: The Armoury · z_acMbzNfoQ · [05:59] · "there's a little bit too much space in the waist"
  - on screen: yes (sizes 46, 48, 50 tried on, [04:53]–[06:32])
  - others: GQ (bLcGL6rKBBg [07:32]) too big looks boxy (R41, dropped).
  - agrees with the four: yes on too small (Jaye, Jz9QVWgBbOg, [13:11], "skin tight jackets"; creators-rules A2). On too big, yes only as "allowed when meant" (Jaye, Jz9QVWgBbOg, [13:11], "Nothing wrong with a boxy suit if that's what you're going for"; Tim, vQ6_okzfNSs, [04:16], "explore some wider fitting suits").
- **R10. Jacket length: about half jacket, half trouser, or a touch less jacket. The knuckle rule fails on long arms.** · part: proportion · strength: nuance
  - holds when / doesn't: a classic suit. See R43 and R52 for the "too short" claims, which clash with the four.
  - interacts with: sleeve length (R11): a sleeve that's too long makes the jacket look longer than it is.
  - source: The Armoury · z_acMbzNfoQ · [06:32] · "Half Jacket half trouser"
  - on screen: yes
  - others: Kwan (pQctf3wAlpI [03:49]) covers the seat; GQ (bLcGL6rKBBg [08:44]) no fixed rule, look at the person.

### Interactions
- Shoulder and chest width make the waist look shaped (R1–R3) [02:11].
- Turn-up weight + not-narrow leg + no break = one long straight line (R5–R7) [03:17]–[03:48].

### Season
- None.

### Ladder
- Bad: too small (pulls, narrow shoulder). Okay: a relaxed size up ("you can get away with it"). Good: the right size with small tweaks. Great: shoulder breadth, chest drape, an implied waist, a high-rise, not-narrow, no-break trouser with weight at the hem.

## A Master Tailor's Guide To The Perfect Suit [4fb4GyOylK8] (2025-01-22) · GQ (Ian Meiers)

### Rules

  - interacts with: Condition: creases on a linen suit are the cloth, but they still read as creases.

- **R17. Navy is the most versatile suit: worn as a full suit, or the jacket alone with jeans, or anywhere between.** · part: color · strength: lift
  - holds when / doesn't: versatility advice; navy jacket with jeans counts as a separate (not Tailored by TL1).
  - interacts with: occasion; jeans (see the clash with the app's [STY-3] below).
