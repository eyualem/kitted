## Across this batch

**What gets said most.**
- **Drape decides how a piece looks** (FlQ3WZ-H2Ik [10:08], [11:12], [11:43], [12:15]; ove4zof6W2k [08:41], [13:38]–[14:12]): R11, R13, R21, R25, R26. He calls it make-or-break for every piece. It's the batch's most-repeated idea and the least visible in a photo.
- **A piece's shape is its details together:** crop, shoulder, sleeve length, hem for the tee (R8); length, width, sleeve, cuff, waistband, shoulder seam, drape for the hoodie (R20, R22).
- **Fit first, and the pants most of all:** R4, R9, R16 (the tee), R31–R33 (the pants can't be cheated).
- **Size is not a substitute for fit:** big, cropped or flashy by default looks swollen (R18); buying "the boxiest T-shirt ever" is lazy (R16); oversized works only when chosen, and stops before dress length (R10).
- **Worn-in beats stiff-new:** an aged tee fits better (R14), broken-in leather becomes the best shoe (R27), patina can't be beaten (R35), looking old carries value (R34), a seasoned hoodie ([01:03]). The only condition fault in this batch is **stiffness** (R12, and the structured hoodie as taste, R26). **He never names worn-out (holes, stains, pilling, stretched collars) as a fault in these seven videos,** and crisp *new* is fine when it isn't stiff (R17).

**Strongest interactions.**
- Drape × cut: drape makes fitted and oversized both flattering (R11, R13).
- Fabric weight × drop shoulder: stiff fabric gives broad structured shoulders and a wider silhouette; soft fabric lets them fall (R26).
- Armhole room × sleeve width: the big sleeve is made at the armpit (R24).
- Layer × tee fit: a layer hides a tee's fit, never the pant's (R31).
- Size × length: sizing up is a new shape until it reads as a dress (R10).

**Pairs with below.** Almost nothing. The tee and hoodie videos never name a pant. The only rules: the right pant works with every top and shoe (R32), and pants fit matters more than the top's (R31).
