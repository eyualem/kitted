## How to read this

- **Labels.** [Study] = peer-reviewed or academic. [Editorial] = a menswear magazine or blog with no product of its own in the piece. [Affiliate] = editorial with affiliate links or a shop roundup. [Retailer] = a shop's own blog. Retailer and affiliate pages are weaker: they gain when you buy more.
- **"Search summary"** means I couldn't open the page and the claim comes from the search engine's summary of it. Treat those as unverified.
- **Pages I couldn't open:** GQ and Esquire block this tool entirely (none of their pages could be searched or opened). Mr Porter Journal, Permanent Style, the Styleforum journal and PubMed Central returned 403 or a captcha. Mr Porter and Permanent Style claims below are search summaries only. The org's LibKey connector wasn't available in this session, so academic links are the publisher or open-access URLs.
- **Dates.** Most editorial pages are 2024–2026 or were updated then. Older ones are marked; they are used only for principles that haven't changed.
- **Visibility estimates** are my arithmetic, not a measurement. In a full-body upload (768×1024 px) a 1.8 m man is about 850 px tall, so about **0.47 px per mm**. A 3 mm chain is about 1.5 px wide, a 6 mm chain about 3 px, a 35 mm belt about 16 px, a 40 mm watch face about 19 px, a ring band about 2 px. The upper-body and lower-body close-ups (#104) add pixels, but I didn't measure how many.
- **The head blur (#54)** covers 1.6 × 1.9 the face box. It probably stops just above the collarbone. Short chains, neck bandanas and scarf knots sit at its edge, so they need checking on real photos before any rule leans on them.

## Bottom line

1. **Layers: the sources agree on what makes one work.** A layer should differ clearly from the top under it in lightness or texture, fit over it without bulk, and usually be the longest piece. "Clearly different or the same, never nearly" applies to layers too. None of the sources praises a layer just for being there. This supports the v5 plan to pay a layer for contrast, not presence.
2. **Accessories: the consensus is restraint.** One or two pieces, one area of the body in the lead, no logos for prep and minimal. Every source treats accessories as optional. **Most accessories can't be seen at 1024 px:** rings, bracelets, thin chains, metal color, and usually watches (one hand holds the phone in a mirror selfie). The ones that can be seen are chunky chains, scarves, neck bandanas, crossbody bags and visible socks.
3. **Color: one controlled study shows moderate matching beats both full matching and clashing.** In Gray et al. 2014, a men's outfit is rated most fashionable at a middle level of coordination. Editorial sources add the "tie-in" (one color repeated in two separate pieces). Together they support a small, code-computed tie-in credit, and no credit for matching everything.
4. **Cohesion: the rule is one idea and one formality level.** A mix reads as intentional when the casual piece is clean and fitted, the base is well cut, and the mix sits in one or two pieces (the shoes or the outer layer). That matches the v4 ramps, so little new is needed.
5. **Good vs. great.** Every judging framework I found names three things: fit, a cohesive idea, and "attention to detail" or "finishing touches". The "one statement piece, everything else quiet" rule appears in 4+ sources. I found **no** source that says what share of outfits experts call excellent. One warning sign: in AesRec 2026 a VLM judge put more than three-quarters of outfits above 8.0.

## 1. Layering: when a layer helps

### What the sources say
- **Contrast in lightness or color with the top underneath.** Ape to Gentleman's layering combos (images dated Dec 2024) explain why each pair works by contrast: a dark olive overshirt over a bright white tee, a grey hoodie under a camel overcoat. [Editorial] ([Ape to Gentleman, layering combos](https://www.apetogentleman.com/best-mens-layering-combos/))
- **Go lighter underneath, darker on top, as a starting point.** The Modest Man (Ian Chandler, updated Jul 8, 2026) says to go from lighter to darker and from thin to heavy, and calls both starting points, not laws. [Affiliate] ([The Modest Man, layering without bulk](https://www.themodestman.com/how-to-layer-without-looking-bulky/))
- **Near-matching solids look wrong.** Gentleman's Gazette (Christopher Lee, Oct 2018) says two similar but not identical solids look off, and repeating a color ties layers together. [Retailer: GG sells its own accessories] ([Gentleman's Gazette, how to layer](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/how-to-layer-clothes/))
- **Each layer should add something different, usually a fabric.** Mr Porter's layering guide says each layer should "bring a different character", most often through fabric. (Search summary; page 403.) [Retailer: Mr Porter is a shop] ([Mr Porter, How To Layer Like A Pro](https://www.mrporter.com/en-us/journal/fashion/how-to-layer-clothes-25105698))
- **Texture is how tonal layers avoid looking flat.** Real Men Real Style (updated Jul 25, 2025) says to vary fabrics and weaves so pieces in the same color don't look flat, and to keep one bright accent at most. [Editorial] ([RMRS, 10 layering rules](https://www.realmenrealstyle.com/style-layer-rules/)) Ape to Gentleman pairs soft knit with rough oxford cloth, and leather with a crisp tee. Derek Guy (Die, Workwear!) argues across many posts that texture does the job of pattern in casual clothes. [Editorial] ([Die, Workwear!, Casual Textures, 2014](https://dieworkwear.com/2014/10/22/casual-textures/); [Knitwear is Better Textured, 2017](https://dieworkwear.com/2017/11/15/knitwear-is-better-textured/))
- **Dissent on texture: don't stack several loud textures.** Dappered (2012) warns that a rough tweed over lambswool over corduroy is too much. (Search summary.) [Editorial, old] ([Dappered, 3 layering rules](https://dappered.com/2012/09/how-to-layer-three-basic-rules-for-getting-it-right/))
- **A tonal layer can work, through texture.** A charcoal hoodie under a black jacket works because the textures differ (youlookfab, search summary, weak source). A Permanent Style "tonal exercise" credits knit-on-knit and matte against shiny for the interest (search summary). ([Permanent Style, tonal exercise](https://www.permanentstyle.com/?p=97088))
- **Length: the outer layer is usually the longest.** Put This On (Pete Anderson, 2013) gives the standard casual formula: the tee or shirt ends around the belt and the jacket is a bit longer. A deliberate break from it works only when it is precise: "Making sure it doesn't look like an accident is key." [Editorial, old but on principle] ([Put This On, Layering Lengths](https://putthison.com/?p=13146)) Harry Rosen says the longest piece should be the outer one, vests excepted (search summary; the page body didn't load). [Retailer] ([Harry Rosen, layering guide](https://www.harryrosen.com/en/editorial/harrys-guide-to-layering))
- **Dissent on length: a little peek is fine.** Menswear Online (2025, a retailer) says a slightly longer shirt showing below a sweater or jacket can look sharp. [Retailer] ([Menswear Online, layering guide](https://www.menswearonline.co.uk/?p=318338)) The Modest Man lists a visible undershirt under an open button-up or flannel as a rule you can break. [Affiliate] ([The Modest Man, rules to break, Jul 2026](https://www.themodestman.com/fashion-rules/))
- **Number of layers: two or three visible.** The Modest Man says no more than three visible layers (an undershirt doesn't count). Menswear Online says two or three. **Dissent:** RMRS says three is right and two is "too simple". Ape to Gentleman's eight combos are all two pieces.
- **Fit and bulk.** Layers go thin to thick. The outer layer must close over what's under it but not be oversized (The Modest Man's test: if you can't button it, it's too small). A chunky sweater under a denim jacket "competes" with it (Ape to Gentleman). RMRS adds that each layer should look good on its own: "If one layer can't look good on its own…don't bother wearing it."
- **Overshirt over a hoodie: endorsed, with conditions.** Highsnobiety (Riccardo Zazzini, 2025) suggests the overshirt slouched open over a hoodie, or buttoned so its texture shows. [Affiliate roundup] ([Highsnobiety, best overshirts](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/best-overshirts/)) Ape to Gentleman names "over a hoodie in spring" as a main use of an overshirt and suggests neutral overshirts (navy, khaki, beige) (search summary). ([Ape to Gentleman, overshirts](https://www.apetogentleman.com/essential-overshirts-men/)) Retailer guides add: thinner fabrics so it isn't bulky, the hood outside, and one of the two pieces plain. (Square Mile and Percival, search summaries; Percival is a retailer.) ([Square Mile](https://squaremile.com/article/overshirt-styling-tips/); [Percival](https://www.percivalclo.com/blogs/style-guides/how-to-wear-an-overshirt)) **No source called it a mistake.**
- **Hoodie under tailoring: high-low, fine if clean and fine-gauge.** Ape to Gentleman (Davey Brett): "Fit and material are what separate contemporary style from rebellious teen". A fine, luxe hoodie goes under a soft-shouldered jacket. [Editorial] ([Ape to Gentleman, how to wear a hoodie](https://www.apetogentleman.com/how-to-wear-a-hoodie-men/)) FashionBeans (Jul 2025) recommends neutral heavyweight hoodies and no loud graphics. [Editorial] ([FashionBeans, upgrade a hoodie](https://www.fashionbeans.com/article/easiest-way-to-upgrade-a-hoodie))
- **Weather.** RMRS treats layering as essential in the cold. **No source scores a layer on warm days.** The #160 "no layer tip on warm days" rule is consistent with them, but nothing here supports a penalty.

## 2. Accessories by style

### General rules
- **How many: one or two, with one area in the lead.** Ape to Gentleman's SS26 trends say minimal jewelry, no more than "two pieces per outfit". [Editorial] ([Ape to Gentleman, accessories SS26](https://www.apetogentleman.com/mens-accessories-trends-spring/)) Primer (Andrew Snavely, 2025) says to pick one area to lead (wrist, neck or fingers) and keep the others quiet. Its 3–5 pieces are a collection you rotate, not what you wear at once. [Affiliate] ([Primer, jewelry guide](https://www.primermagazine.com/2025/learn/mens-jewelry-guide)) Ape's minimalist guide says one or two pieces. [Affiliate] ([Ape to Gentleman, minimalist accessories](https://www.apetogentleman.com/minimalist-accessories-men/)) Mr Porter says that even a peacock should stop at two (search summary). ([Mr Porter, man-bling](https://www.mrporter.com/en-pl/journal/fashion/how-to-carry-off-man-bling-750944)) InsideHook allows two necklaces at most and two rings per hand (search summary). ([InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/style/guide-wearing-mens-jewelry)) Die, Workwear! (2018) warns against piling on pieces to make an outfit "pop". [Editorial] ([Die, Workwear!, men and jewelry](https://dieworkwear.com/2018/06/20/the-controversial-issue-of-men-jewelry/))
  - **Dissent:** jewelry retailers suggest 3–5 pieces in a wrist stack (Trendhim, Robinsons; [Retailer], search summaries).
- **What reads as too much or cheap.** Big or ornate pendants, skulls, slogans and logos, jewelry that jingles, oversized beads (Primer 2025). Novelty pieces (Die, Workwear!). Chains "too heavy" read as a caricature, too thin as dainty (Ape chain guide, search summary). ([Ape to Gentleman, chains](https://www.apetogentleman.com/chain-necklaces-for-men/))
- **Jewelry and tailoring don't mix.** Die, Workwear! compares jewelry with a suit to "painting a mustache on the Mona Lisa". Jewelry suits workwear, denim and contemporary casual clothes. Retail guides add: subtle at work, bold in the evening (search summaries).
- **Mixing metals: sources split.** "Agree" (one metal family): Mr Porter (search summary), Primer's belt piece (the buckle matches the watch), and the Modest Man ring guide's traditional rule ("mix only on purpose"). "Mix freely": The Modest Man's rules-to-break (2026) and Primer's jewelry guide (2025). **3 vs 2. Not scorable either way** (see visibility).
- **Belt and shoes.** Primer (Stillman Brown, Aug 14, 2025): the belt should "correlate" with the shoe color (browns with browns, blacks with blacks), at a similar formality. [Editorial] ([Primer, 13 belt questions](https://www.primermagazine.com/2020/spend/mens-belt-questions)) RMRS: mixing black and brown is always a beginner's mistake; the rule relaxes as the outfit gets more casual. [Editorial] ([RMRS, belt and shoes](https://www.realmenrealstyle.com/matching-shoes-belt/)) Permanent Style: closely related, not identical, and with casual shoes there's much less need to match (search summary). ([Permanent Style, belt vs shoes](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2018/09/should-your-belt-match-your-shoes-or-bag.html)) The Modest Man 15-accessories list: match tone to the shoes. **Dissent:** The Modest Man's rules-to-break: "Your belt and shoes should look good together." Navy, grey and olive belts are fine, and a belt can be skipped when it isn't needed. Ape's 2026 outdated rules (already in `color.md`) say belt and shoes needn't match exactly. Sneakers are exempt everywhere.
  - **Net:** 5 sources say "same color family, never black with brown, with leather shoes"; 2 say "complement". Nobody wants an exact match.

### By accessory
- **Chains.** Over a plain tee, a chain is the simplest way to make a basic outfit look styled (Ape chain guide, search summary). The thickness sweet spot is 2–6 mm at 18–22 in (Ape). Silver is easiest with grey, navy and olive (Ape). Streetwear came from hip-hop "bling", now refined (Highsnobiety chain roundup [Affiliate], [link](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/best-chain-necklaces-buy-online/)). Beginners should wear a light chain (Primer). Die, Workwear! notes that gold chains carry social connotations.
- **Watch.** The most accepted single piece: clean dial, no oversized face, fits the wrist (The Modest Man 15 accessories, May 2026 [Affiliate], [link](https://www.themodestman.com/15-subtle-accessories-that-elevate-mens-outfits/)). Ape's SS26 list says low-profile and dress watches are rising and chunky ones fading.
- **Bracelets and rings.** Slim leather or a box chain. One ring to start. Keep the dominant hand freer (The Modest Man ring guides, [link](https://www.themodestman.com/how-to-wear-rings/)). Silver cuffs suit workwear and Western clothes (Die, Workwear!, search summary).
- **Bandana or neckerchief.** Put This On (Derek Guy, Apr 2021) says bandanas "go most naturally" with workwear, in thin fabric and neutral or complementary colors. A contributor in the same piece warns they can look "affected or dandy". [Editorial] ([Put This On, bandana guys](https://putthison.com/theres-an-alarming-number-of-bandana-guys/)) Ape SS26 lists the neckerchief as a 2026 trend, at the neck or tied to a bag. Heddels covers bandana buying, not styling ([link](https://www.heddels.com/2018/06/buyers-guide-bandanas/)).
- **Scarf.** Classic colors (charcoal, navy, camel), thinner wool or cotton, cold weather (The Modest Man). RMRS counts scarves as a layer.
- **Bag.** Crossbody or sling for casual, streetwear, techwear and outdoor looks: nylon for casual, leather for smart (Ape to Gentleman bag guides [Affiliate], [link](https://www.apetogentleman.com/best-crossbody-sling-bags/); Highsnobiety crossbody roundup [Affiliate], [link](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/best-mens-crossbody-bags/)). Ape SS26: big, soft, slouchy messenger bags. **The evidence is product roundups, not judgment of outfits.**
- **Socks.** Show socks on purpose or hide them. No-show socks with loafers and low sneakers. Never white athletic socks with dress shoes (The Modest Man). White socks are a casual or Ivy choice and seasonal (Put This On, search summary, [link](https://www.putthison.com/the-unlikely-return-of-white-socks/)). White sport socks with shorts and a retro runner are a recognized summer look (Ape, search summary). Football socks belong to blokecore (Highsnobiety, [link](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/euros-2024-blokecore/)).
- **Caps and beanies (can't be scored: the head is hidden).** For the record: neutral, logo-free, textured caps for smart casual (Ape, Permanent Style search summaries), cuffless logo-free beanies (Ape SS26), never with a suit. These stay out of v5.
- **Logos.** One or two visible at a time (The Modest Man rules-to-break). No flashy logos for prep and minimal (Ape minimalist; The Modest Man old-money and preppy guides, [link](https://www.themodestman.com/old-money-aesthetic/)). v4 already handles logos.

## 3. Color

Already sourced in `notes/color.md` (not repeated): light/dark separation between pieces, the near-match rule, "neutral base + one accent", Schloss & Palmer 2011 on hue similarity and lightness contrast, O'Donovan & Agarwala 2011 (2–3 hues, no support for color-wheel templates), warm/cool as weak. New here:

### Evidence (studies)
- **Gray et al. 2014, "The Science of Style: In Fashion, Colors Should Match Only Moderately" (PLOS ONE).** 239 online raters each saw 30 outfit color combinations from one of four palettes (two men's, two women's). "Coordination" was set by separate ratings of each pair of swatches. For men's outfits the straight-line trend was not significant, and the inverted U was (R² = .28). The most fashionable outfits were **moderately** coordinated; fully matched and fully different both rated lower. For women's outfits the curve "accounted for twice as much variance as the linear effect". [Study] ([PLOS ONE 10.1371/journal.pone.0102772](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0102772)) *Limits:* drawings or swatch outfits, online raters of all ages (mean 35), not photos of real men. The author list wasn't shown on the page I opened; the citation reads "Gray et al., 2014".
- **Fashion and Textiles (2025), vol. 12, art. 27:** a psychophysical experiment on how people today judge color harmony in clothing combinations. It found that judgments "differed significantly from previous theories", then trained ML models. A table on the page shows tonal and tone-in-tone rules scoring highest. [Study] I read the abstract and one table only; the full text redirected to a login. ([Fashion and Textiles](https://fashionandtextiles.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40691-025-00433-y))
- **A 2016 Acta Optica Sinica garment-harmony study** reports that existing color-harmony models predicted garment harmony poorly (R² < 0.5) (search summary). [Study] ([researching.cn](https://m.researching.cn/articles/OJ16e4c1a3ea1c7f01)) This supports the existing decision not to build color-wheel scoring.
- **Color alone predicts outfit compatibility about as well as full image features** (Learning Color Compatibility in Fashion Outfits, arXiv 2020; already noted in `scoring_evidence.md`). [Study] ([arXiv 2007.02388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02388v1))

### Editorial
- **Tie-ins (one color repeated in two separate pieces).** Gentleman's Gazette: "The subtle repetition of a color connects layers and unifies your look." RMRS's 3-color piece suggests belts and brown shoes "to tie the look together", and its multi-color-accessory trick makes other pieces echo one of its colors (search summary). [Editorial] ([RMRS, 3-color outfits, updated Mar 2026](https://www.realmenrealstyle.com/3-color-outfits/)) The Outfit Narrative "sandwich" (same color top and bottom with a different one between) is already in `color.md`.
- **Tonal outfits need a range of shades or texture.** The Modest Man's all-black guide says to mix fabrics because black absorbs light, and to add charcoal for depth (search summary). [Affiliate] ([The Modest Man, all black](https://www.themodestman.com/how-to-wear-all-black/)) Ape to Gentleman's color combos (about May 2024) call tonal grey "tricky" and suggest charcoal with light grey, and say brown-on-beige must keep the separation "clean and crisp". [Editorial] ([Ape to Gentleman, colour combinations](https://www.apetogentleman.com/outfit-colour-combinations/)) Styleforum says tonal works best in earth tones and with close but not identical tones (search summary; page 403). ([Styleforum journal](https://journal.styleforum.net/art-tonal-layering))
- **Number of colors: about three main colors.** RMRS calls three "a robust guideline". It counts neutrals inconsistently: brown belt and shoes act as a neutral in one example and a fourth color in another.

## 4. Cohesion: mixing styles and formality

- **One idea, one formality level.** Derek Guy (Put This On, Feb 2018): an outfit should "express one idea", like a thesis, and "Dress according to one level of formality." Mixing highs and lows is possible but takes great attention to detail, so beginners should avoid it. [Editorial] ([Put This On, a successful outfit](https://putthison.com/how-to-put-together-a-successful-outfit/))
- **A mix reads as intentional when it's deliberate, clean and well fitted.** FashionBeans (Apr 2017, updated May 2024): "the balance needs to appear deliberate". Battered trainers with a suit, or bootcut jeans with dress shoes, read as accidents. Fit is non-negotiable. Keep the base simple when the sneakers are the statement. [Editorial] ([FashionBeans, high-low](https://www.fashionbeans.com/2017/best-high-low-outfit-combinations/))
- **Change the outer pieces and accessories, not the core, and keep the two ends close.** Permanent Style's high/low piece: play with accessories and outerwear, and don't push the high and the low too far apart (a Barbour with a casual suit, not with black tie) (search summary; page 403). ([Permanent Style, high/low dressing](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2018/04/highlow-dressing))
- **The hoodie under a camel overcoat** is the stock example of a high-low that works (Ape to Gentleman). A clean hoodie is a precondition (Ape hoodie guide).
- **Head-to-toe theme reads as costume; swap one piece instead.** Ape to Gentleman's gorpcore guide: the full kit is overdone, so mix one or two outdoor pieces into normal clothes (search summary). ([Ape to Gentleman, gorpcore](https://www.apetogentleman.com/gorpcore-trend/)) Ape says to keep corduroy to one piece per outfit so it doesn't look themed (search summary). Highsnobiety: treat the football jersey as the statement and keep the rest simple. Mr Porter's workwear "costume" warning is already in the RULEBOOK sources.
- **Context decides.** Permanent Style's sprezzatura piece: effortlessness becomes artifice when everyone around you is doing it (search summary). ([Permanent Style, sprezzatura](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2017/05/what-is-sprezzatura))

## 5. What separates good from great

### Frameworks that judge outfits
- **Racing "Best Suited" contests (Victoria Racing Club, Perth Racing).** Judges score originality, appropriateness, understanding of current trends, accessories, grooming, and whether the whole outfit fits together. Attention to detail "will be assessed across all elements", including accessories, footwear and finishing touches. Perth uses a panel of three. (Search summaries of VRC and Perth pages.) ([VRC submission guide](https://www.vrc.com.au/fashions-on-the-field/news/best-dressed-submission-guide/); [Perth Racing](https://www.perthracing.org.au/venues-and-events/best-dress-derby-day))
- **4-H "Fun with Fashion" scorecard.** The only numeric rubric I found: 100 points across six items, fit worth 15 (search summary). A youth contest. ([UT Extension PDF](https://marshall.tennessee.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/60/2020/01/Fun-with-Fashion-Scorecard.pdf))
- **JamaisVulgaire "Style Rater" (2024, a paid product).** Six criteria: cut and proportion (the biggest effect), color harmony, fabric, accessories and details (each piece's formality should match), context, and sprezzatura ("Too much looks sloppy. Not enough looks like a wedding costume."). No weights published. [Retailer: sells the rater] ([style-rater.jamaisvulgaire.com](https://style-rater.jamaisvulgaire.com/))
- **AesRec (2026).** Three experts scored 100 outfits on eight dimensions: design and tailoring, color and pattern, material and texture, craftsmanship detail, comfort, complementary matching, overall coordination, overall. No rubric definitions and no expert score distribution are published. **The VLM's own scores averaged 8.15, with more than three-quarters above 8.0.** [Study] ([arXiv 2602.03416](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03416v1)) That is a direct warning against letting a model judge the level.

### The "standout piece"
- **One statement piece, the rest quiet.** Ape to Gentleman's style tips say only one statement piece per outfit (search summary; [link](https://www.apetogentleman.com/mens-style-tips-should-know/)). The Modest Man: a graphic tee works when it's the statement and the rest is the frame (rules-to-break, 2026). Highsnobiety: the jersey is the statement. Put This On: accessories add a "point of distinction", but watch for things that draw too much attention. **4 sources, no dissent.** This backs v4's point of interest (K3) and "too many focal points" as they are.
- **Texture or one good detail as the standout for quiet outfits.** Derek Guy across posts: a camp collar, a deeper placket, a striped shirt, or a better cloth carries a plain outfit; plain knits are failsafe but "devoid of personality" (search summary of several posts). PS tonal: interest comes from texture. This backs v4's "vary texture or shade" tip for minimal outfits, and suggests texture by garment type (C3) can be a minimal outfit's standout.

### What share of outfits experts call excellent
- **No editorial or academic source gives one.** The contests pick one winner per field. AesRec doesn't publish its experts' distribution. The council's target (about 20–25% at 8+, ≤5% at 9+) stays an internal assumption until R1/R2 and the gold set.

### What this means for v5 (recommendations)
1. **Rebuild "Layered" on L1 + L2 + L3:** full credit for a clear lightness step *or* a texture-class change by garment type; half for a weaker step; nothing for a near-match. L1 and L2 need only the `lightness` reading already planned for the v4 batch, or the phone's measured lightness. That makes it the cheapest strong rule.
2. **Add the tie-in (C1) as the main non-layer route to an 8**, computed in code from color families or measured color. It gives no-layer streetwear and summer outfits a path the sources support.
3. **Make "finished" one credit from big pieces only** (chunky chain, scarf or neck bandana, crossbody bag, deliberate socks), inside the cap. Leave watches, bracelets, rings, metals and belts out of the score: they can't be read reliably at 1024 px. The belt/shoe black-brown rule stays a tip.
4. **Use G1 for 9+** once C1 and C3 exist. That fixes the RULEBOOK objection that requiring a standout would empty 9+.
5. **Flip-test first:** `lightness`, `neckwear`, `chain thickness`, `socks_visible`, `bag`, and the L4 hem peek, in that order (#63–64 lesson). Check on real photos whether the head blur clips chains and neckwear.
6. **Don't let a model judge the level.** AesRec's VLM put more than 75% of outfits above 8; the level comes from people's ratings (R1–R3, gold set).
