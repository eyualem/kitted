## 1. Silhouette shapes and which volume combos read as balanced in 2025–2026

### Takeaway
The 2025–2026 consensus is "contrast or control volume, don't max it everywhere": a wide leg wants a top that is relaxed but not huge (or short), and a boxy top wants a narrower or straight leg. But sources disagree on how fitted the top should be under a wide leg: one says "close to the body," another says "the shirt needs some volume too." The safe reading for scoring is to penalize only the extremes: skin-tight top over a very wide leg, and oversized top over skinny pants.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 leg-opening bands (hem width): skinny 5.5–6.5", slim 6.75–7.5", straight 7.5–8.25", relaxed/slightly wide 8.5–9.5", wide 9.5–11", baggy/pleated 11"+. RMRS (June 22, 2026) — [RMRS, slim vs wide trousers](https://www.realmenrealstyle.com/slim-vs-wide-trousers/)
- RMRS says most men should "land in the straight or relaxed-straight middle ground for 2026," with about a 9" hem as the sweet spot. — [RMRS](https://www.realmenrealstyle.com/slim-vs-wide-trousers/)
- Wide leg needs some top volume: "your shirt needs some volume too"; pair relaxed oxfords or oversized knits; avoid skin-tight henleys. — [RMRS (2026)](https://www.realmenrealstyle.com/slim-vs-wide-trousers/)
- Opposite view, the contrast rule: "if the pants are wide, the top half must be close to the body"; a boxy overshirt or oversized hoodie needs a narrower leg; going loose on both "is the one way this goes wrong"; "One loose piece, one fitted piece is the rule." (Snippet. The search engine linked it to the H&M Men's Fall Trends 2026 editorial, but the page returned 403, so the exact source isn't confirmed.) — [H&M Men's Fall Trends 2026 (snippet)](https://www2.hm.com/en_us/editorial/inspiration/men-autumn-trends.html)
- The same snippet's escape clause for all-loose outfits: "the fix is length rather than width." A cropped jacket over baggy jeans works because the jacket ends at the hip. — [H&M Fall 2026 (snippet)](https://www2.hm.com/en_us/editorial/inspiration/men-autumn-trends.html)
- Trend direction: fuller trouser cuts and deliberate proportions in 2025–2026; one aggregator reports "wide leg trousers men" searches up about 180% year on year (secondary, unverified figure). — [Aza Fashions blog, June 18, 2026](https://www.azafashions.com/blog/2026/06/18/mens-traditional-pant-styles-trends/)
- Retail confirms the wide era: H&M Fall 2025 denim is built around baggy, loose straight and relaxed straight cuts; baggy fits "drop the crotch, and stack at the ankle." — [The Fashionisto, H&M Fall 2025 jeans](https://www.thefashionisto.com/?p=1510441)
- Inverted "A" silhouettes are legitimate when deliberate. Put This On (2013) shows a shorter jacket over a longer shirt making an A shape instead of the usual V. "Making sure it doesn't look like an accident is key." — [Put This On, Real People: Layering Lengths, Sep 19, 2013 (older)](https://putthison.com/?p=13146)
- A garment-level fit fault on the shoulders: a garment that fits the body "but hangs off your shoulders" creates an unbalanced look. (Snippet; the source is one of the Art of Manliness / Stylerave / Moss pages, exact one unclear.) — [Art of Manliness, men's fashion body type (snippet)](https://www.artofmanliness.com/style/clothing/mens-fashion-body-type/)
- One 2026 guide (for bigger builds) rejects the contrast approach. It recommends a "controlled middle ground" (regular top + straight leg) over mixing fitted and loose. Its framing is body-based (out of scope), but the garment combo it favors is "regular + straight." — [Veroshape, June 18, 2026](https://veroshape.com/blogs/news/style-guide-bigger-builds-men-2026)

**Agreement count on "oversized top + skinny/slim pant = unbalanced":** 3 supporting (H&M snippet, RMRS's logic that volume should match, the Put This On "deliberate or it looks like an accident" principle); 0 conflicting. Kitted's −0.3 is well supported.
**Agreement on "everything oversized = fault":** 1 supporting (H&M snippet), 1 partly conflicting (the same snippet allows it if the top layer is short), plus the 2025 streetwear norm of baggy + stack (Fashionisto). Kitted's "outside streetwear/gorpcore" carve-out is right. Consider adding a second exemption: all-loose is fine when the top layer is cropped or at the belt.
**Agreement on "fitted/skin-tight top + wide leg = fault":** 1 supporting (RMRS), 1 conflicting (H&M snippet says the top should be "close"). Recommend no penalty for fitted (volume 2) + wide; penalize only volume 1 (skin-tight) + baggy/wide, and only lightly.

### Inferences
- A two-axis volume table (top volume 1–5 × leg shape) gets you most of the way. Penalize the corners (5 + skinny, 1 + wide/baggy) and both-max outside streetwear, unless the top is short. Everything in the middle band is fine. Signals: **HAVE** (volume, leg_shape, length).
- The "V vs A vs H/I" vocabulary doesn't add scorable signal beyond that table. The A shape (short top over wide leg) is the only one that needs a length check, and the length field already covers it.

## 2. Rule of thirds, rise, tucking, top length, cropped jackets

### Takeaway
The rule of thirds (top third / legs two-thirds, or the reverse) is the most-cited proportion principle. Stylists get there in two ways: (a) by moving the visual waistline up (tuck, front tuck, high rise, cropped/waist-length top), or (b) by a deliberately long top over a slim leg. The fault is an untucked top that ends near the halfway point (below the crotch on regular tops), which splits the figure 1:1. Kitted's current tip (front tuck or cropped layer when the top ends mid-fly) is consistent with this. But sources treat a mid-fly hem on a regular tee as the correct default length, not a fault.

### Cited Findings
- Legs should take up roughly two-thirds of visual height, the upper body about one third; trousers at the natural waist (at or near the navel) create this. — [Westwood Hart, Natural waist trousers, Apr 5, 2026](https://westwoodhart.com/blogs/westwood-hart/natural-waist-trousers-rise-fit-proportions-men)
- How to get a 1/3 : 2/3 split: a cropped, boxy top or a waist-length jacket with a higher-waisted, full-cut trouser. (Snippet from Westwood Hart's suit-proportions guide.) — [Westwood Hart, suit proportions (snippet)](https://westwoodhart.com/blogs/westwood-hart/guide-better-suit-proportions-menswear-fit-cut-tailored-jacket-trousers)
- Tucking or front-tucking a top with full-length pants creates a visual break that moves you toward the 1/3 : 2/3 split. (Women's-wear-leaning source; the principle is gender-neutral.) — [A Well Styled Life, Rule of Thirds (snippet)](https://awellstyledlife.com/the-rule-of-thirds-for-outfits/)
- Cropped length "breaks your body into 1/3 at the top," so pair it with wider-leg bottoms to carry the bottom 2/3. (Snippet.) — [H&M Fall 2026 / cropped-jacket guides (snippet)](https://www2.hm.com/en_us/editorial/inspiration/men-autumn-trends.html)
- Default untucked tee length: the hem should fall "around mid-zipper or just past your belt line." — [Menswear Online, Aug 27, 2025](https://www.menswearonline.co.uk/?p=305195)
- Same default from a second tee guide: the hem at the middle of the fly. (Snippet.) — [SANVT, How should a T-shirt fit (snippet)](https://sanvt.com/journal/how-should-a-t-shirt-fit/)
- Front ("French") tuck: tuck the front only, leave the back loose; it adds structure and shows the belt line. — [Menswear Online, 2025](https://www.menswearonline.co.uk/?p=305195)
- Oversized tees play by different length rules; Menswear Online says the hem lands around mid-thigh on purpose, "not past the knees." (Mid-thigh seems long; treat as one source.) — [Menswear Online, 2025](https://www.menswearonline.co.uk/?p=305195)
- Tailoring alignment: a single-breasted jacket's button point should line up with the trouser waistband (natural waist). — [Westwood Hart, 2026](https://westwoodhart.com/blogs/westwood-hart/natural-waist-trousers-rise-fit-proportions-men)
- Traditional casual default: shirt "ending just below the beltline," with a jacket "just a little longer." — [Put This On, 2013 (older)](https://putthison.com/?p=13146)

**Agreement:** rule of thirds as a principle: 4 supporting (Westwood Hart ×2, A Well Styled Life, H&M snippet), 0 conflicting. "Mid-fly is the correct length for a regular untucked tee": 2 supporting (Menswear Online, SANVT), 0 conflicting. "Below-crotch untucked regular top = fault": implied by all length guides; no source defends it except as an oversized/streetwear look.

### Inferences
- The highest-value length rule is **"regular-volume (1–3) untucked top ending below the crotch = mistake,"** unless the leg is slim or skinny on purpose and the top is a long-line or oversized streetwear tee. Signals: **HAVE** (length=below_crotch, volume, tucked, leg_shape).
- Mid-fly on a regular tee should stay **neutral** (a tip at most). Credit should go to a deliberate waist break: tuck or front tuck with a mid/high rise, or a cropped/at_belt top over a straight/relaxed/wide leg. **HAVE**, except rise.
- **Rise** (low/mid/high) matters to the thirds rule but is only visible when tucked or with a short top. Proposed **NEW** field: `rise` (low/mid/high/unknown), read only when the waistband is visible; otherwise "unknown," and per Kitted's partial-check rule it never costs points.
- Sources say cropped tops pair best with wider legs. A cropped boxy top over skinny pants is a fault candidate (top-heavy and short). Moderate confidence: the only support is the H&M snippet's logic. **HAVE.**

### Gaps
- No source quantified how far below the crotch is "too long" for a regular tee (from a photo it's ordinal anyway).
- No 2025+ men's source found that defends low-rise as a deliberate look (Y2K revival); not checked.

## 3. Layer-length rules (inner vs outer, hoodie under overcoat, shirt under sweater, coat length)

### Takeaway
The traditional rule is outer layer equal to or slightly longer than the inner. Inner layers peeking out below the outer hem (shirt tail under a sweater, hoodie under a coat) are accepted as a deliberate casual layering look, but only when the reveal is small and even. A random long tail reads as "accident." Overcoat-length rules are timeless tailoring: about mid-thigh to knee, and never ending at the widest part of the calf. A coat must be long enough to cover the jacket under it.

### Cited Findings
- Traditional: shirt ends just below the belt, jacket "just a little longer." — [Put This On, 2013 (older)](https://putthison.com/?p=13146)
- Breaking it is fine if deliberate: a denim midlayer clearly shorter than the shirt, a short tee over a longer tee, with "precise" sleeve length and calm colors so it "doesn't look like an accident." — [Put This On, 2013 (older)](https://putthison.com/?p=13146)
- Tailored jacket length (timeless): the hem grazes about the thumb knuckle ("rule of thumb"), or covers at least three-quarters of the seat. (Snippet.) — [Put This On, Rule of Thumb](https://putthison.com/?p=15363); [Black Lapel, How long should a suit jacket be (snippet)](https://blacklapel.com/blogs/the-compass/how-long-should-a-suit-jacket-be)
- Overcoats mostly sit mid-thigh so they cover a sport coat or suit jacket underneath. (Snippet; source likely Harry Rosen, page didn't load.) — [Harry Rosen, How long should your winter coat be (snippet)](https://harryrosen.com/en/editorial/how-long-should-your-winter-coat-be)
- Coat-length guidance: about 1" above the knee keeps the trouser line; longer coats are OK but should not end at the widest part of the calf (it draws a horizontal line). The snippet ties this to height (out of scope); the "don't end at the widest calf" part is garment-only. (Snippet.) — [Harry Rosen (snippet)](https://harryrosen.com/en/editorial/how-long-should-your-winter-coat-be)
- Cropped outer layers over long or baggy bottoms work because the outer ends at the hip. (Snippet.) — [H&M Fall 2026 (snippet)](https://www2.hm.com/en_us/editorial/inspiration/men-autumn-trends.html)

**Agreement:** "Outer layer shorter than a tailored jacket under it (overcoat/topcoat shorter than the blazer) = fault": 2 supporting (Harry Rosen snippet, Put This On rule of thumb logic), 0 conflicting. Timeless. "Inner showing below outer = fault": 1 supporting as default (Put This On), 1 conflicting (same source shows deliberate versions). Trend-dependent: tolerated more in the 2020s.

### Inferences
- Scorable rules, highest confidence first:
  1. **Tailored coat/topcoat shorter than the blazer or sport coat under it → mistake.** Signal: `shows_below_outer` on a blazer under a coat. **HAVE** (if blazer-under-coat is detected).
  2. **Blazer/sport coat with a casual shirt tail hanging below the jacket hem → mistake** (smart-casual). **HAVE** (shows_below_outer + the inner's length).
  3. **Hoodie or sweatshirt hem visible below an overcoat → neutral** (an accepted 2020s casual look); a hood over the collar is the point of the look. No penalty. **HAVE.**
  4. **Shirt hem 1–3" below a crewneck sweater → neutral or tiny credit** (the classic preppy reveal); a long tail (below the crotch) → small mistake. Needs the inner's length as well as the outer's; probably **HAVE** if each layer gets a length.
- Proposed **NEW** field: `outer_length` for coats/jackets (cropped/at_hip/covers_seat/mid_thigh/knee/below_knee/calf). This enables "coat ends at mid-calf" (minor) and "coat shorter than the blazer under it" (stronger). The calf point is a weak, timeless rule; I'd make it a tip only.

### Gaps
- No 2025+ source found on hoodie-under-overcoat rules specifically; the "neutral" call is an inference from the general 2020s layering norm, not cited.
- Couldn't load Harry Rosen; coat-length figures rest on snippets.

## 4. Shoe weight vs. trouser leg width

### Takeaway
This is one of the best-agreed proportion rules: shoe visual weight should scale with leg volume. Wide legs need substantial shoes (lug boots, chunky derbies, chunky/retro sneakers, heavy loafers); slim legs want sleek shoes. Thin minimalist sneakers or pointy dress shoes "disappear" under a wide hem. Kitted already penalizes chunky shoes with skinny/slim pants (−0.45) but has no rule for the inverse (dainty shoe under a wide leg), which sources flag just as strongly.

### Cited Findings
- "Wide-leg trousers need shoes with visual weight and presence"; thin, dainty shoes "get swallowed up." — [RMRS, best shoes for wide-leg trousers, June 18, 2026](https://www.realmenrealstyle.com/best-shoes-wide-leg-trousers-men/)
- Avoid with wide legs: pointy dress shoes, ultra-thin minimalist sneakers ("If your sneaker disappears under the hem, it's the wrong sneaker"), square toes, basketball high-tops (they bunch at the ankle), flip-flops/slides. — [RMRS, 2026](https://www.realmenrealstyle.com/best-shoes-wide-leg-trousers-men/)
- Recommended with wide legs: penny/horsebit loafers, derbies, chunky lug boots, clean retro sneakers (Samba, Gazelle), suede chukkas. Note: RMRS counts low-profile retro sneakers as fine, so "chunky" isn't required, just "not tiny." — [RMRS, 2026](https://www.realmenrealstyle.com/best-shoes-wide-leg-trousers-men/)
- Shoe-to-leg ladder: slim → sleek leather, slim sneakers, chelsea boots; straight → most shoes; relaxed-straight → heavier derbies, beefroll loafers, suede chukkas; wide → lug boots, heavy loafers, chunky sneakers. — [RMRS, slim vs wide, 2026](https://www.realmenrealstyle.com/slim-vs-wide-trousers/)
- Chunky sneakers' "bulkier design" complements wide legs (New Balance 990, Hoka-type runners named). (Snippet.) — [Opumo, what shoes with wide leg pants (snippet)](https://www.opumo.com/magazine/what-shoes-to-wear-with-wide-leg-pants-men/)
- Platform/chunky soles lift the hem so the pant drapes better. (Snippet; retailer source.) — [Vessi blog (snippet)](https://ca.vessi.com/blogs/the-forecast/what-shoes-to-wear-with-wide-leg-pants)
- Baggy-denim stack logic: the leg opening should "swallow the collar" of the sneaker. (Low-quality aggregator; the trend claim only.) — [Programming Insider, baggy denim (low quality)](https://programminginsider.com/the-death-of-skinny-jeans-why-narrative-driven-baggy-denim-is-the-future-of-menswear/)

**Agreement:** "Wide leg + dainty/minimal/pointy shoe = unbalanced": 3 supporting (RMRS ×2, Opumo snippet), 0 conflicting. "Slim leg + chunky shoe = unbalanced": 1 direct (RMRS ladder, implicit), 0 conflicting; it's also the classic "clown shoe" complaint from the slim era. Trend dependence: the wide-leg half is a 2025–26 rule; the slim-leg half is timeless.

### Inferences
- Add the inverse rule: **wide/baggy leg + sleek/minimal shoe (thin-sole minimalist sneaker, pointy or sleek dress shoe) → mistake (~−0.3).** Needs a **NEW** shoe field: `shoe_profile` (sleek/regular/chunky). Kitted's existing "chunky sneakers/runners" type check covers only one end. Retro low-profile sneakers (Samba-type) count as "regular" and are fine with both.
- Keep the chunky + skinny/slim penalty, but consider limiting it to skinny + chunky, or slim + very chunky. RMRS lists "heavier derbies" even for relaxed-straight, and slim + regular sneakers is fine. The −0.45 size has no source behind it; it is a product choice.
- Basketball high-tops + wide leg: a weaker, single-source fault (bunching). Skip.

### Gaps
- No source rates how much this matters compared with other faults.

## 5. Hem/break vs. leg shape; cuffs, stacking, cropped trousers; shorts length

### Takeaway
Hem rules depend on leg shape. Slim/straight trousers: little or no break, or a slight break. Wide trousers: one clean break, minimal sock showing (RMRS), or a deliberate cropped wide trouser. Baggy streetwear denim: stacking is intended. Pooling on tailored or smart-casual trousers is a fault in every source. Shorts consensus for 2025–26: inseam about 5–9" with 7" the sweet spot, ending above the knee; inseam advice is often tied to height (out of scope).

### Cited Findings
- Wide/relaxed trousers: "One clean break at the shoe. No stack. No floor-sweep." — [RMRS, 2026](https://www.realmenrealstyle.com/slim-vs-wide-trousers/)
- Wide trousers should break cleanly on the shoe "with little to no exposed sock when standing." — [RMRS, 2026](https://www.realmenrealstyle.com/best-shoes-wide-leg-trousers-men/)
- Streetwear baggy jeans "stack at the ankle" by design (H&M Fall 2025 Fairfax baggy fit). — [The Fashionisto, H&M Fall 2025](https://www.thefashionisto.com/?p=1510441)
- Traditional casual default: straight or slightly tapered pants with "a slight break." — [Put This On, 2013 (older)](https://putthison.com/?p=13146)
- Shorts: 2–3" above the knee, 7–8" inseam for most men; 7–9" broadly; 7" = "today's sweet spot" at mid-thigh; 5" is fine for warm weather; 9" sits at or just above the knee (traditional). Snippets also give height-based inseam advice (out of scope). — [Mizzen+Main (snippet)](https://www.mizzenandmain.com/blog/how-long-should-mens-shorts-be); [American Eagle blog, Aug 4, 2026 (snippet)](https://blog.ae.com/2026/08/04/how-long-should-your-shorts-be-the-mens-shorts-inseam-guide/amp/); [Opumo (snippet)](https://www.opumo.com/magazine/how-long-should-my-shorts-be-a-modern-guide-to-getting-it-right/)

**Agreement:** "Pooling on smart-casual/tailored trousers = fault": 3 supporting (RMRS ×2, Put This On), 0 conflicting. "Stacking on baggy denim = OK": 1 supporting (Fashionisto/H&M), 1 conflicting for trousers (RMRS's "No stack," but that is about trousers, not streetwear denim). "Shorts ending above the knee": 3 supporting, 0 conflicting, for classic/smart-casual. The 2025–26 longer baggy jorts below the knee were not checked (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Hem × leg-shape matrix (all **HAVE**: hem + leg_shape + garment type):
  - Skinny/slim + pooling → mistake. Slim + no_break/slight_break/cropped → fine. Slim + cuffed → fine.
  - Straight + slight/full break → fine; pooling → mistake.
  - Wide/relaxed *trousers or chinos* + pooling → mistake; + cropped (ankle showing) → fine as a deliberate look (Japanese/workwear wide crop). It conflicts with RMRS's "little to no sock," so score it neutral, not as credit.
  - Baggy/wide *jeans* in streetwear style + pooling/stacked → neutral (intended).
- The current `hem` enum lacks a "stacked" value separate from "pooling." Proposed **NEW** enum value: `stacked` (folds above the shoe, hem not dragging on the ground) vs `pooling` (fabric collapsing over the shoe or floor). It is a meaningful difference in the wide era.
- Shorts: proposed **NEW** field `shorts_end` (high_thigh/mid_thigh/just_above_knee/at_knee/below_knee). Rule: classic/smart-casual shorts ending below the knee → small mistake, except in streetwear (jorts, basketball shorts). Low-to-moderate confidence.

### Gaps
- No 2025+ editorial source found on below-knee baggy jorts as a deliberate look; worth a Codex TikTok check before penalizing below-knee shorts.
- Cuffs: no 2025+ source found with cuff rules by leg width (old tailoring lore says cuffs suit fuller, pleated trousers; not cited here).

## 6. Shoulder fit and sleeve length as proportion signals

### Takeaway
On regular-fit tops the shoulder seam should sit at the shoulder edge and tee sleeves should end around mid-bicep. Seams falling down the arm and sleeves reaching the elbow signal "too big" unless the tee is clearly designed oversized (drop shoulder). These are the most common beginner fit faults that a single photo can show, but they read as fit/size rather than proportion. Kitted's `sleeve_end` covers sleeves; shoulder position isn't captured.

### Cited Findings
- Shoulder seams "should align neatly at your shoulder's edge"; short sleeves shouldn't go past mid-bicep. — [Menswear Online, 2025](https://www.menswearonline.co.uk/?p=305195)
- Sleeve end at mid-bicep, not near the elbow (that reads oversized); seam on the shoulder bone, "not sagging off the shoulder." (Snippet.) — [SANVT, How should a T-shirt fit (snippet)](https://sanvt.com/journal/how-should-a-t-shirt-fit/); [Beige Brown T-shirt fit guide (snippet)](https://www.beigebrown.com/pages/men-s-t-shirt-fit-guide)
- A garment that fits the body but "hangs off your shoulders" looks unbalanced. (Snippet.) — [Art of Manliness (snippet)](https://www.artofmanliness.com/style/clothing/mens-fashion-body-type/)
- Deliberate proportion play needs "precise" sleeve length to avoid looking accidental. — [Put This On, 2013 (older)](https://putthison.com/?p=13146)

**Agreement:** 4 supporting, 0 conflicting for regular-fit tops. Trend-dependent: oversized/drop-shoulder tees (volume 4–5) are exempt by design in 2025–26.

### Inferences
- Proposed **NEW** field: `shoulder_seam` (on_shoulder/slightly_dropped/far_dropped/unknown). Rule: **volume 2–3 (regular) top with far_dropped shoulders or elbow-length sleeves → "too big" mistake**. That is a size mismatch, not an intended oversized look. Volume 4–5 → exempt. Note the circularity risk: the model may label any dropped-shoulder tee as volume 4. The rule only works if "volume" describes the cut, separately from "fits him."
- Long-sleeve/shirt sleeves past the knuckles or bunched heavily at the wrist → small fault; **HAVE** via `sleeve_end` if its enum covers past-wrist.

### Gaps
- No source compares shoulder-fit faults with length or shoe faults in viewer impact.

## 7. Which proportion faults matter most to an average viewer; does the golden ratio hold up?

### Takeaway
I found no study that ranks proportion faults by how "put together" lay viewers rate an outfit. Editorial consensus points to visible length/size errors (pooling hems, below-crotch untucked tops, sleeves past knuckles, shoulders hanging off) and volume extremes (oversized top + skinny pants; dainty shoe under wide leg) as the most noticed. The golden ratio does not hold up as a universal aesthetic law. The "rule of thirds" is a stylist heuristic, not a measured optimum.

### Cited Findings
- McManus's paired-comparison studies: group preference for the golden-section rectangle is weak. Individual preferences are strong and stable but vary widely between people; with bias-free methods a universal golden-ratio preference "might in fact not exist." — [Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 2016 review (PMC4937063)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4937063)
- Body-attractiveness research with standardized clothing finds several ratios matter (golden proportion, waist-to-hip ratio, BMI). The golden ratio's role is mixed: some studies show correlation, others none or very weak. (Snippet; these concern bodies, out of scope for Kitted.) — [PMC3485848 (snippet)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3485848); [PolyU thesis record (snippet)](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/handle/10397/83161)
- Proportion play only works when it doesn't look accidental. Precision (sleeve length, calm color) is what separates styled from sloppy. — [Put This On, 2013 (older)](https://putthison.com/?p=13146)

### Inferences
- Avoid "golden ratio" in app copy or rubric justification. Say "about a third / two-thirds" as a stylist rule of thumb.
- Suggested priority for Kitted (my ranking, not sourced data; it weighs source agreement and how visible the fault is):
  1. Pooling hem on non-streetwear trousers (HAVE)
  2. Regular untucked top ending below the crotch (HAVE)
  3. Oversized top + skinny/slim pant (HAVE; already −0.3)
  4. Wide/baggy leg + sleek/minimal shoe (NEW `shoe_profile`)
  5. Regular-cut top too big: shoulders far dropped or sleeves to the elbow (NEW `shoulder_seam`; partly HAVE via sleeve_end)
  6. Chunky shoe + skinny pant (HAVE; already −0.45; maybe soften for slim)
  7. Coat shorter than the blazer under it / shirt tail below a blazer (HAVE)
  8. Everything oversized outside streetwear unless the top is short (HAVE; add the length exemption)
- Credits: a deliberate waist break (tuck/front tuck, or a cropped/at_belt top over straight/relaxed/wide legs) is the best-supported credit (rule of thirds, 4 sources). Shoe weight matched to leg width could be a small credit (3 sources).

### Gaps
- No empirical (survey or lab) data on lay perception of men's outfit proportion faults. The ranking above is inference. Kitted's gold-set human calibration would be the right way to test it.
- GQ, Esquire, Highsnobiety, FashionBeans, The Modest Man and Gentleman's Gazette 2025–26 pages were not reached within the time budget; Mr Porter blocked the fetcher.
