# Market size, trends and free substitutes for Kitted

Research date: **Oct 12, 2026**. Scope: (1) how many US men 18–30 Kitted could reach, bottom-up; (2) 2025–2026 trends; (3) what ChatGPT, Gemini, Google, Pinterest, Amazon, Meta and TikTok now do for "rate my outfit / what should I wear". Dedicated closet and AI-stylist apps are covered in the sibling file `market/competitors-adjacent.md` and are not repeated here.

**How to read this file**
- Every number has a source and a date. **"(snippet, unverified)"** = seen only in a search summary or a secondary write-up, not on the primary page. **"(company-reported)"** = the company's own claim.
- **Estimate** and **Judgement** are labelled. Everything else is a sourced fact.
- Biggest gap found: **there is no representative, men-only US survey (2024–2026) on how many young men want to dress better.** The "style-motivated" share below is an assumption, anchored on weaker proxies. Say so whenever it's quoted.

| SOM ($, gross) | payers × $29.99 | ~$13k | **~$67k** | ~$337k | Net to you is about 15% less if Apple's Small Business Program rate applies (verify enrollment). Monthly-plan payers ($5.99) would raise revenue per payer. |

What the table says (judgement):
- **The market is not the constraint.** Even the mid SOM reaches only about 1.6% of the mid SAM. Year 1 is limited by distribution (TikTok reach) and free-to-paid conversion, not by how many guys exist.
- **Year-1 revenue at the mid case is part-time-income scale (~$60k gross).** Reaching ~$300k needs either a viral loop (roughly 250k downloads) or conversion in the top quartile. A real free tier keeps conversion near the freemium median (2.1%), not the hard-paywall median (10.7%, RevenueCat 2026). That's the price of "never charge to see a score".
- Worth checking: the 18+ gate removes the 87%-iPhone teen market, which is the biggest pool of iPhone-heavy users. That's the right call for legal reasons (see CLAUDE.md), but it costs reach.

## 2. Trends (2025–2026)

### 2.1 What young men are wearing
- **Away from logo streetwear, toward "dressed-up casual".** Lyst data (shared with CNN, Jan 15, 2026): global quarter-zip demand **+31% in Q4 2025**, +7% YoY; Saint Laurent's Le Loafer searches **+66%/month** on average in Q3 2025. Coverage frames 2026 as loafers, derbies and boots replacing sneakers, plus overshirts, knitwear and relaxed tailoring. Lyst's Katy Lubin called the quarter-zip "quiet luxury's final victory lap".
- **The quarter-zip is a Gen Z men's TikTok phenomenon,** partly read as dressing for a tough job market (Fortune, Nov 20, 2025; NPR, Dec 5, 2025). A Nov 2025 TikTok of two 22-year-olds in quarter-zips passed 30M views (CNN via KRDO, Jan 15, 2026). Fortune cites a Gen Z male jobless rate of ~9.1% in Q2 2025.
- **Workwear is the strongest search signal.** Pinterest Men's Trend Report 2026 (FashionUnited, Sep 2026): "vintage workwear outfit men" **+442%**, "workwear outfit men" +314%, M-65 field jacket +148%, "men chain necklace" +351%, "pink shirt outfit men aesthetic" +338%. Pinterest Predicts 2026 (Dec 2025): "field jacket outfit men" +65%, "baggy suit" +90%. These are percent changes in Pinterest searches, not sales.
- **Loose fits held.** Levi's said men's and women's loose fits were each **up 40%+** in Q1 2024 (earnings call). Retail editorial in 2026 still lists baggy and barrel jeans, while MR PORTER (2026) warns against going too baggy and recommends a relaxed straight leg. I found no 2025–2026 primary sales split by leg shape.
- **Japanese "city boy"** (Popeye magazine; wide, relaxed silhouettes, earth tones, prep + workwear + Americana) is an established reference among style-literate young men. I found **no 2025–2026 quantitative data** on it. Gorpcore and "old money" also have no fresh numbers; the quiet-luxury thread now shows up mainly as the quarter-zip / loafer story.
- **For Kitted (judgement):** the styles Kitted already scores (workwear, smart casual, minimal, classic prep, streetwear, vintage sport, outdoor) match where the search growth is. Workwear and smart casual are the rising ones; logo streetwear is the falling one.

### 2.2 The "fit check" format and men's style creators
- "Fit check" is a mainstream Gen Z video format: show the outfit, often in 360°, and name the brands (Dictionary.com pop-culture entry).
- I found **no reliable 2025–2026 hashtag view count** for #fitcheck; TikTok's Creative Center is the place to pull one. The men's quarter-zip wave above shows the format working for young men at tens of millions of views.
- Advice demand is large and old: r/malefashionadvice had **6.3M members** (Aug 2025, Wikipedia).
- **Judgement:** fit-check videos are Kitted's natural marketing format (score reveal = hook). The founder's daily posting fits the channel where this audience finds brands. BCG (Oct 2025) found Gen Z/Alpha are **1.5×** as likely as older shoppers to discover brands on social, and micro-influencers (22%) are nearly as influential as celebrities (27%).

### 2.3 Looksmaxxing and the backlash
- **Awareness is high among young men:** in a UK poll, 40% of men 18–34 knew the term vs 6% of men 35+ (*The Rest is Entertainment* "Vibe Check" poll, cited by YPulse Apr 6, 2026 (snippet, unverified)).
- **Spending signal:** 68% of US Gen Z men (18–27) used facial skincare in 2024, up from 42% two years earlier (Mintel, via search summary (snippet, unverified)); US men's grooming sales topped $7.1B in 2025, +6.9% (NielsenIQ, via CNBC Jan 10, 2026 (snippet, unverified)).
- **Face-rating apps already monetize this.** Fortune (Jul 1, 2024): Umax rates faces out of 100, $3.99/week, 7M+ downloads, founder-claimed ~$500k/month (Fortune couldn't verify).
- **Backlash grew in 2026:** doctors warned about DIY "bone smashing" and fillers after the trend's best-known influencer was hospitalized during an April 2026 livestream (multiple outlets, Apr 2026). Psychologists link the trend to male body dysmorphia (Fortune 2024; *Lancet Child & Adolescent Health* 2025 review, cited secondhand).
- **For Kitted (judgement):** a big opportunity *and* a reputational risk. Young men want to improve how they look, but "rate me" now carries a looksmaxxing taint. Kitted's rule "never rate faces, bodies, weight or skin", plus faces blurred before upload, is a real differentiator worth saying out loud in marketing ("we rate the outfit, never you"). Avoid any "maxxing" vocabulary.

### 2.4 AI in fashion shopping (Gen Z)
- **41% of Gen Z + Gen Alpha use AI weekly to shop for fashion,** vs 34% of older generations (BCG + WWD, Oct 29, 2025, 9,000+ US consumers). BCG calls fashion their "entry point" to AI.
- **38% of US consumers had used gen AI for online shopping;** 26% used it for virtual try-on (Adobe survey, 5,000+ US consumers, via Digital Commerce 360, Aug 21, 2025).
- **AI referral traffic to US retail sites +693.4% YoY in holiday 2025** (Adobe, Jan 7, 2026). Apparel was *not* among the top AI-shopping categories (games, toys, appliances, electronics and personal care were). The base is still small.
- **ChatGPT usage mix:** "Practical Guidance" is ~29% of messages, and "Health, Fitness, Beauty, or Self-Care" is its own category. Users 18–25 send ~46% of messages from users who state their age. The paper has **no fashion category**, so there's **no official number for outfit advice** (Chatterji et al., OpenAI/NBER *How People Use ChatGPT*, Sep 15, 2025).
- **No survey I found measures how many people use ChatGPT for outfit advice.** Vogue Business (May 2025, via IADS) and Fast Company reported it as a growing Gen Z habit, without numbers. YPulse: 44% of 13–39-year-olds are interested in an AI personal shopping assistant (Aug 2024 report, via YPulse May 15, 2025).
- McKinsey/BoF *State of Fashion 2026* (Nov 2025) names AI shopping agents as a top theme: shoppers use LLMs for discovery and "style advice" (summary via secondary coverage; McKinsey page timed out).

### 2.5 How people feel about AI judging appearance
- **46.2%** of US adults 18–50 believe an AI shopping assistant would give more *honest* outfit advice than a friend (Bloomreach, Jun 12, 2025; two surveys of ~1,000 each). Vendor-commissioned.
- **25%** would trust an AI trained on their purchases to pick an outfit over a friend; higher for younger shoppers (Algolia survey of 1,000 US adults, via Sourcing Journal / Yahoo (snippet, unverified; date and generation split conflict between write-ups)).
- **Trust is lowest where the result is personal and visible to others** (Harris Poll on beauty AI; qualitative).
- **Gen Z sentiment is souring:** excitement about AI fell 14 points to 22%, and **31% report anger** toward it (Gallup, Apr 8, 2026). Among adults who don't use chatbots, 79% cite concern about personal-data use (Pew, Oct 9, 2026).
- **For Kitted (judgement):** "honest, consistent feedback a friend won't give" is a supported hook. Privacy (faces blurred, no photos stored on the server) and "your stylist" (not "AI stylist") fit the souring mood. Lead with the outfit and the fixes, not the AI.

Overall threat (judgement): **the real substitute is "ask ChatGPT"**, now with try-on and shopping built in. Nobody else offers a repeatable score, a closet learned from your fit photos, one specific fix, and a daily habit for beginner guys. That gap is real but narrow: one OpenAI or Instagram feature ("Rate my outfit" with memory) could close much of it for casual users.

## 4. Implications for Kitted (judgement, ranked)

1. **Sell repeatability and specificity, the things ChatGPT can't promise.** "Same photo, same score", the five-part breakdown, and "one fix that moves you from 6.5 to 7.5" are the defensible features. In demos, show the same fit sent to ChatGPT twice (different answers) next to Kitted (same score). The one-photo-two-scores problem is also the first thing a skeptical reviewer would test.
2. **Lead with privacy and "we rate the outfit, never you".** The looksmaxxing backlash and Gen Z's growing anger at AI (Gallup 2026) make this a selling point, not small print. It's already built (#54–55).
3. **Don't compete on try-on or shopping.** Google, OpenAI, Perplexity, Amazon and TikTok are all spending there, for free. Keep "Should I buy this?" (v1.1) focused on *your closet* ("goes with 6 things you own"), which none of them can see.
4. **Ride the "dressing for success" wave in content.** Quarter-zips, loafers, workwear and interview and first-job outfits are where young men's attention went in 2025–2026. Kitted's smart casual and workwear paths to 8 fit that. Video hooks: "Rating my quarter-zip fit", "Interview outfit: 6.5 → 8.0 with one swap".
5. **Expect freemium conversion around 2%, and plan for it.** The real free tier is a principle, but it puts year-1 paid users at roughly 400–11,000 (estimate). The levers are the 14-day trial on the annual plan (RevenueCat 2026: 17–32-day trials convert at a 42.5% median trial-to-paid), the founding offer for the waitlist, and getting more downloads, not a bigger market.
6. **Watch three launches:** a native "rate my fit" in ChatGPT, outfit rating or ideas inside Instagram (Meta's Muse Spark shopping), and Google bringing Doppl-style closet features into Search. Any of these would weaken the free-tier pull.
7. **Fill the evidence gap yourself.** No good survey says how many young men want style help. The waitlist and the TikTok comments ("what would you score this?") are the cheapest way to measure it. Log sign-up conversion by video.
