# Kitted icon finalists (Oct 14)

Three finalists, each as `final-N.svg` (light), `final-N-dark.svg`, `final-N-tint.svg` (white shapes on transparent, for iOS 26 tinted mode), with 1024 px PNGs (tint PNGs are rendered on grey). All shapes, no text or fonts. The generator is `gen.py` here (`/usr/bin/python3 gen.py`); PNGs and `test-sheet.png` are rendered with headless Chrome. `test-sheet.html` shows each one at 180/60/40/29 pt, dark and tinted, in an App Store "rate my outfit" results list beside 3 generic rival stand-ins, as a round TikTok avatar (100 and 40 px), and at 60 pt next to two generic crowded-K stand-ins.

Shared: indigo gradient `#22305C → #1A2448` (dark `#1A2448 → #0C0E14`), the faint twill from round 1, the two-piece K from brand-4 (stem plus a separate chevron), at most three layers (background with one flat shade, one chalk shape, one copper shape), no shadows or glows. Both "context" shades (pocket opening, neck opening) are a flat darker tone that's part of the background.

## final-1 · Button on the pocket
- **What changed from brand-1:** the coin is now a button sitting on a jeans front-pocket scoop. One solid chalk band (78 px) drops in from the top edge at upper left, bends round in a quarter circle and runs under the button and out the right edge. The area inside the scoop (upper right) is a flat darker indigo, the pocket opening. The button is 55% of the icon: flat copper, one thick deep-copper groove (44 px), a flat lighter-copper half-moon at upper left for the dome, and the two-piece K stamped in indigo `#1A2448` (not deep copper on copper).
- **Tried and dropped:** the seam *ending* at the button (with a gap round it). It read as a medal on a ribbon or a pendant on a chain. Letting the seam run on past the button, plus the darker pocket opening, makes it read as fabric with a button on the seam. A dashed seam was not used: at 29 pt the dashes turn to dots, so it's a solid band as the brief allowed.
- **At 29 pt:** a copper disc with a white curve above it at the top left; the curve still reads as a curve. The K inside is a dark mark, not a letter, at that size. Strongest silhouette and colour of the three.
- **Tinted:** a white disc with the groove and K cut out, a white curve, and the pocket opening at 10% white. A small gap round the button keeps it apart from the seam. Clean.
- **Risks:** still a round copper thing with a K, so a quick look can say "coin"; the seam is what argues "jeans", and it's thin at 29 pt. Front-pocket scoop only: no Arcuate (the back-pocket double curve) and no tab, so it stays clear of Levi's. In a round avatar the button sits off-centre and the seam is cut by the circle; for TikTok use a centred button-only crop.

## final-2 · Woven label
- **What changed from brand-2:** no folded corner. The label is seen sewn just under a neckline: the neck opening is a darker band at the top and the chalk label follows the collar's curve (the drape). It's ~71% of the width. Each sewn end has a row of 4 bold copper stitches (30 px wide, 56 px long, rounded, slightly deeper copper `#CF7C43` so they hold on chalk), lying along the end. The two-piece K is indigo.
- **Tried and dropped:** a straight or tilted rectangle with fat stitch bars or fold strips. It read as a ticket or a film strip. The curve is what makes it a label in a garment; elongated stitches (not square ones) stopped the sprocket-hole look.
- **At 29 pt:** a curved white band with a navy K and warm ticks at each end. Reads as "a K on a label"; the stitches shrink to texture.
- **Tinted:** a white curved label with the K and stitches cut out, the neck opening at 10%. Clear.
- **Risks:** the curved band plus the dark top can read as a waistband or a bowl at a glance; the K carries recognition, and plain K's are crowded (in the K check it's the only one with a shape around the K). Least colourful of the three on a home screen: the copper is small.

## final-3 · Label + check
- **What changed:** the panel's merge. The same neck label (no K) with indigo stitches, and one bold copper check (104 px, square ends, sharp corner, so never a swoosh). The check starts on the label, its corner sits on the label's bottom edge, and the long arm leaves the label and runs up into the neck opening. A 20 px gap separates the check from the label. Copper is used only for the check.
- **Keeping it off to-do apps:** the check isn't inside a box or circle; it sits on a curved label in a collar with stitched ends, and it breaks out of the label. That's the most this concept can do.
- **At 29 pt:** a copper check on a white curved shape. It reads "checked" first, "clothes" second.
- **Tinted:** the white label with the stitches and a gap cut out, and a white check. Clear.
- **Risks:** the highest "generic check / task app" risk of the three. Next to other check icons it is the least ownable, and the check says "done", not "here's your fix". No K, so it doesn't carry the name.

## Recommendation
**final-1** is the strongest icon (one copper shape, best at 29–60 pt, the brand line literally). **final-2** is the best "it's about clothes" read and the best brand mark for labels, share cards and the wordmark. final-3 explains the app fastest but looks the most like other apps.
