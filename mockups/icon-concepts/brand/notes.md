# Kitted icon concepts: the brand mark (Oct 14)

Four concepts that build on the wordmark's pieces: the K, the woven neck label, the dashed copper stitch and the copper rivet. Each one comes as `brand-N.svg` (light), `brand-N-dark.svg` and `brand-N-tint.svg` (white shapes on transparent, for iOS tinted/mono mode), with a PNG render of each. The tint PNGs are rendered on grey so the white shows up. Everything is drawn as shapes (no text, fonts or images).

How they're built (per the lead's research notes): an indigo background with a soft gradient `#22305C → #1A2448` (dark mode: `#1A2448 → #0C0E14`) and a very faint twill (bold diagonal bands, white at 3.5%). At most 3 layers: background, main shape, copper accent. No baked-in shadows or glows. Copper is always one bold solid shape, and stitch strokes are 30 px or more with dashes of 64 px or more. The K is never a plain bold K: in three of the four concepts it's cut as **two pieces** (a stem and a separate chevron with a gap), so the "Kitted K" is visibly made of parts, like a garment.

## 1. Shank Button (`brand-1`)
- **Idea:** a domed copper jeans button with the two-piece K and a ring stamped into it in deep copper, on raw-denim indigo.
- **Why it fits:** it's the lead's top pick (a domed copper rivet), and it is the literal "one copper rivet" of the brand line. Nobody else in the category uses anything sewn. The stamped K is the brand's own cut, not a font.
- **At 60 px:** a copper coin on navy with a dark K. It reads instantly, and it's the strongest silhouette of the four. Risk: copper with a K could recall KAYAK's copper K; the ring, the dome and the indigo keep it closer to "jeans button".
- **Tinted:** the button becomes a solid white disc with the ring and K cut out (the K shows faintly, at 28%), over a faint twill. It's recognisable as a button with a K.

## 2. Folded Label (`brand-2`)
- **Idea:** the woven neck label in chalk, with copper stitch rows at each end. Its top-right corner is folded back to show a solid copper underside. The two-piece K is in indigo.
- **Why it fits:** it's the wordmark's woven label turned into an object you've touched in every shirt. The fold gives the one copper shape and a bit of life.
- **At 60 px:** a white tag with a navy K and a copper corner. The stitches turn into small copper ticks. Second most readable; the fold is what tells it apart from a generic "K card".
- **Tinted:** a white label with the K and stitches cut out, and the fold at 50% white, so the folded corner still reads.

## 3. The Seam (`brand-3`)
- **Idea:** two panels of denim sewn on the diagonal. The K's upper arm *is* the seam: a row of big copper running stitches that runs past the K, edge to edge.
- **Why it fits:** the most custom K of the four ("a K whose arm is a stitched seam"). It reads as made, not typed, and the stitch line has movement.
- **At 60 px:** a white stem and leg with a copper dotted diagonal. It reads as a K with a stitch. Weakest of the four at 40 px, where the stitches shrink to dots.
- **Tinted:** a white stem and leg with the stitches at 62% white, and the upper-left panel as a faint 10% tone. Still a K.

## 4. Bar Tack (`brand-4`)
- **Idea:** a large white two-piece K (stem and chevron with a clear gap) joined by one thick copper bar tack, the dense stitch that reinforces jeans pockets.
- **Why it fits:** it's the plainest form of "the K is constructed". It works as an app icon and as a standalone brand mark (avatar, watermark on share cards), and it pairs with the wordmark.
- **At 60 px:** a big white K with a copper bar through its joint. Very legible. The gap between the two pieces fades below about 60 px, so it then reads as a K with a copper dash.
- **Tinted:** a white K with a cut-out halo around the tack, and the tack at 75% white, so the joint stays visible without colour.

## Recommendation
**1 (Shank Button)** is the strongest icon: one shape, copper as hero, best at 40–60 px, and the lead's top pick. **4 (Bar Tack)** is the best all-round brand mark, and its two-piece K could be the K used inside 1 and 2, as it is now. 3 is the most original but loses the most when small.
