# Woven label, round b: bold and modern (Oct 14)

Four variants. Each has a light, a `-dark` and a `-tint` version (white shapes and opacity on transparent), all 1024 SVG, shapes only, with PNGs (tint PNGs are drawn on grey). `gen.py` makes the SVGs (`/usr/bin/python3 gen.py`) and `render.sh` makes the PNGs with headless Chrome. `sheet.png` / `sheet.html` show each one at 220, 87, 58 and 29 px, in dark mode, and in a simulated tint.

**Shared.** The old finalist had a twill texture, a collar curve, fat copper dashes and a two-piece K. All of that is gone. Every variant uses flat fills and up to 3 colours: indigo `#1A2448` (`#22305C` and night in dark mode), chalk, and copper (`#DE9762`, or a deeper `#C9733A` where it sits on chalk). There are no gradients, shadows or texture. The new K is one custom outline in the spirit of Archivo Expanded: 1.15 times as wide as it is tall, a heavy stem, flat horizontal ends, and a leg that grows out of the arm rather than the stem. An ink trap was tried and dropped because it looked like a mistake. The stitches are thin, long dashes (22–24 px, about 5:3 on to off) instead of fat beads.

## b1 · The label is the icon
- **Why it's premium:** it's a poster. A full-bleed indigo field, two copper running stitches from top to bottom like the label's sewn ends, and a big chalk K. There is one copper moment and nothing else. Folded-end panels were tried and dropped because they read as a picture frame.
- **At 29 pt:** a K between two fine dotted lines. The K reads, and the stitches turn into a thin copper rule on each side. Below 29 pt it's weaker, because the stitches are only about 1 px.
- **Tinted:** a white K with two dashed lines at 75%. It's the cleanest of the four.
- **Look-alike risk:** without the stitches it's a plain K on dark (the fintech risk), so the stitches must stay. It also hints at a denim seam, which is fine for a clothing app.

## b2 · The tag edge
- **Why it's premium:** a confident band. The chalk band takes half the icon's height and runs off both sides, so it feels bigger than the icon. The indigo K sits left of centre, and a single copper stitch near the right edge suggests the sewn end just beyond it. The off-centre balance is what keeps it from looking like a template.
- **At 29 pt:** the strongest silhouette of the four, a white bar with a dark K. The stitch becomes a tick.
- **Tinted:** a solid white band with the K and stitch cut out. Very recognisable.
- **Look-alike risk:** a white stripe on navy can read as a flag or an airline tail. The stitch is the only thing marking it as a label. The dark version flips it to an indigo band with a chalk K.

## b3 · Copper thread
- **Why it's premium:** a single gesture. The chalk label is tilted 6°. One copper thread starts inside its top edge, runs down the left seam, turns once, then pulls taut under the label and off the right edge. Three other routes were tried and dropped: a thread running past the top, a zigzag tack, and a tail hanging down. All three read as a flag on a pole or a price tag on a string. The taut line below doesn't.
- **At 29 pt:** a small tilted card with a copper corner line. This is the weakest small-size read, because the label is only about 60% of the icon.
- **Tinted:** a white label with the K cut out and a white thread with a thin gap around it. It holds, but it reads a bit like a card with a clip.
- **Look-alike risk:** a tilted card can say "wallet" or "payments" (the fintech look). The copper thread is what argues "clothing".

## b4 · Wordmark crop
- **Why it's premium:** an editorial close-up of the real wordmark. The label's left end sits on a chalk shirt, the copper stitch is just inside it, and there's a huge K followed by the start of the tracked-out I, which the icon edge cuts off. It's the variant that connects most directly to the in-app wordmark and the share card.
- **At 29 pt:** a big K with a light sliver and a copper line on the left. The cut-off I shrinks to a bar at the right edge, which reads as "the word goes on" at 60 pt but is close to noise at 29 pt.
- **Tinted:** a white K and I on a 14% white label with the dashed stitch at 70%. Clear.
- **Look-alike risk:** "KI…" might bring "Kia" to mind for a second, though the shapes are nothing alike. In dark mode the chalk sliver turns to night, so the label's end is quieter.

## Recommendation
b1 makes the cleanest brand mark, and b2 has the strongest home-screen silhouette. b4 is the most ownable if the wordmark becomes the identity. b3 is the most expressive but loses the most at small sizes.
