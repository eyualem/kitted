# Woven label, round 2 (Oct 14): four quiet variants

Files: `aN.svg`, `aN-dark.svg`, `aN-tint.svg` (white with opacity on transparent) and 1024 px PNGs (tint PNGs shown on grey). `sheet.html` / `sheet.png` show each at 180, 60, 40 and 29 pt, on a wallpaper, in dark mode and in a simulated tint. Generator: `/usr/bin/python3 gen.py` (shapes only, no text or fonts). PNGs come from headless Chrome.

**What changed from final-2, all four:** the twill stripes, the neckline curve, the loud orange dashes and the chunky two-piece K are gone. The field is plain indigo with a barely visible top-to-bottom grade (`#22305C → #1D2850`). The label is one flat shape with 2% corners. **The K is new:** a geometric monogram with the arm and leg meeting in one crisp notch that touches the stem (like Gotham, not Helvetica). Weights are 17.6% of the height for the stem, 15% for the arm and 16% for the leg, cut flat at the cap and baseline. It's semibold to match the Archivo Expanded wordmark, but it's drawn, not typed. No shadows, glows or bevels. At most 3 colours (indigo, chalk, copper) plus tones of them.

## a1 · Quiet flat label
- **Why it looks premium:** a chalk label 61% of the icon wide (624 × 284, about 2.2:1 like a real neck label) on plain indigo, with lots of empty space. The only detail is two slim copper stitches (12 × 46 px, deep copper `#9C5326`) at each sewn end, close to the edge where a label is really sewn. It's the same move as A.P.C. and COS labels: small type, big margins, one detail.
- **At 29 pt:** a white bar with a navy K. The stitches disappear, which is fine because they're only for close-up. The label silhouette is the strongest of the four.
- **Dark:** the colours flip. An indigo label on night, a chalk K, warm copper stitches. Nothing glares.
- **Tinted:** a solid white label with the K cut out, and the stitches at 50%. Clear at every size.
- **Risk:** it's the plainest. If it ever looks like a name tag, the stitches are what argue "label".

## a2 · Tone-on-tone
- **Why it looks premium:** a deep indigo label (`#161F3F`) on a lighter indigo field (`#2E3E74`). The K is in a pale indigo thread (`#B9C1DC`) and three of the stitches are tonal (`#3A4778`). There's one copper stitch, on the right end at the bottom: the single mark that's "ours". This is the most restrained, closest to Our Legacy and Acne's tonal labels.
- **At 29 pt:** a dark bar with a light K. It holds because the K is light on dark. The label edge is softer than in a1 but still visible.
- **Dark:** a night label on a dark-indigo field with a lavender K.
- **Tinted:** the label at 30% white with the K at full white, so the tone-on-tone idea survives. The copper stitch is the only full-white stitch.
- **Risk:** it's the quietest on a busy wallpaper, and the field's lighter indigo leans a little purple next to the app's `#1A2448`.

## a3 · Loop label
- **The shape:** the label is folded over a seam. The seam is a darker band across the icon with one fine copper topstitch line along it, stopping short of the label. The part of the label over the band is the fold, a step deeper in tone (`#CFD3DE`). Its lower edge rolls down slightly in the middle, like fabric turning over an edge. The front layer below carries the K.
- **What I tried and dropped:**
  - A back layer peeking out below the front read as a keyboard key, three times over (square, landscape, and with a copper back).
  - A portrait loop with stitches at the fold read as a tear-off calendar.
  - A straight fold line read as a browser title bar.
  - Copper stitches over the K read as an accent mark (a "K̄").
- **Why it looks premium:** it's the only one that shows the label in a garment. It does that with one band and one thread line, not a collar drawing.
- **At 29 pt:** a white label hanging on a dark line. The thread line goes thin but the seam band holds.
- **Dark:** an indigo label on night, a near-black band, a warm copper thread.
- **Tinted:** the band at 16%, the thread at 60%, the fold at 55%, and the front solid with the K cut out. You can still see the layering.
- **Risk:** it's the busiest of the four. Some people may read it as a pocket or a card in a sleeve.

## a4 · Woven texture
- **Why it looks premium:** a1's label with a fine horizontal weave only inside the label: 6 px rows every 14 px in a barely darker chalk (`#E8EAEF` on `#F3F4F6`). Up close, at App Store and 180 pt sizes, it reads as fabric. At 60 pt the rows blend into one flat tone, so it looks like a1. The texture is a reward for looking closely, not decoration you see at a glance.
- **At 29 pt:** the same as a1.
- **Dark:** faint indigo rows on the indigo label.
- **Tinted:** the rows come through as about 60% white stripes inside a solid label, with the K and stitches cut out. It's still clearly a label with a K.
- **Risk:** some Chrome renders show the rows as fine lines, like lined paper, on the large preview. If that bothers you, `#ECEEF2` makes them even quieter.

## My pick
**a1** for the home screen: the best read at 29 pt and the calmest. **a2** if Kitted wants to look more like a fashion brand than an app. **a4** is a1 with a close-up reward, worth it if the App Store page shows the icon large. a3 is the only one that says "garment" without words, but it's the least calm.
