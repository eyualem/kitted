What is he going for?
Three places to ask.

The answer steers the advice and breaks a tie when an outfit reads as two looks. It never changes the score (#213): renaming a look can't game it, and nobody loses points for telling the app more. The ask has to be hard to miss and cost almost nothing, so every option starts with his answer already picked.

App appearance

Where the pre-pick comes from

In order: the look on today's morning card if he opened the camera from it, else the look he confirmed on his last check (within 7 days), else his first onboarding pick, else Not sure. Doing nothing is a valid answer.

What the answer changes

Advice only: which fix leads on a tie, the Next level line, and a plain sentence when the outfit reads as another look. Never the number, the word, or the Why sheet's rows. "Not sure" means: go by what the outfit reads as.

Never in the way

No sheet, no modal, no extra step. The score shows the moment it's ready, whether he answered or not. Every control has a 44 pt hit area (tiles and the Something else / Not sure buttons draw at 44+; chips draw at 36–38 with the rest as padding, like the app's chips). One primary action per card: Re-check stays the only filled button.

Why not real Liquid Glass

03 §4.5 keeps glass for floating controls and off reading text, and inside a solid card glass has nothing behind it to bend, so it would look like a grey box. The inset is a tinted "frost" fill with a 1 pt top highlight: it reads lighter and different from the result card's grey boxes without the costs. With Reduce Transparency it becomes plain surface-2. 03 has no motion tokens yet; the spring above would be the first (motion.inset).

VoiceOver and the rules

When the inset arrives, VoiceOver says once, after the current stage: "While I read it: what are you going for? Minimal is picked, from this morning's card." Focus doesn't move. Tiles are one button each ("Minimal, selected. Plain pieces, quiet colors, clean lines."). Every target is 44 pt or more: tiles 82 pt tall, More 32 drawn with a 44 hit area, the Headed row 44, Change 44 × 44. The Gym and Home occasions show no look question (nothing to aim at).

Option A · v1 · for comparison

AAsked during the 6-second wait

Right after the shot or the library pick, the reading card fills in as today, and a question card sits under it: his three looks as small drawn tiles (the onboarding LookTile, same drawings), plus Something else and Not sure. The occasion question that already fills the wait moves into the same card, so the wait holds one card of questions, not two.

  • One tap answers. No Confirm button. The tile shows its check for a beat, then the card folds to one line: "Going for minimal · Change".
  • The reading card loses only its skeleton numeral while the question is up (about 70 pt), so both cards fit on one screen; the found pieces and the stage list stay.
  • The progress never moves out of view: the stage list stays above the question at every size; at accessibility sizes the question card scrolls under it.
  • If the score lands first, the question card goes away and the result card carries a compact row instead, same answer pre-picked. It's never a blocker: the fixes are already on the card.
  • VoiceOver: the reading card announces "Reading your fit. Question: what are you going for today? Minimal is picked." Each tile is one button, "Minimal, selected. Plain pieces, quiet colors, clean lines."; the drawings are hidden.
Option B

BChips on the camera, while framing

A row of glass chips above the Propped / Mirror switch: the occasion first (it opens its menu), then his looks with the pre-pick filled, scrolling sideways.

  • The catch: in Propped mode he's 2–3 m from the phone when the camera matters, so the chips are out of reach, and the 3-2-1 fires on its own.
  • It also adds a row to the busiest screen in the app, over his photo, next to live tips like "Step into the frame".
  • Useful as a source, not a question: the camera already receives the morning card's occasion and look, and that feeds A's pre-pick.
Option C

C"Judged as a minimal look" in the card header

The result card's top line names the look and opens the same menu as the occasion (MorphPicker, #69): his looks first, then the rest, then Not sure.

  • Easy to miss: it's the same small mono line the founder already called too easy to miss for a chip.
  • "Judged as" says the wrong thing: it promises the score depends on it. It doesn't (#213), so the label invites the gaming the rule exists to stop, and a change that leaves 7.0 at 7.0 feels broken.
  • It only shows after the score, when he's reading fixes, not choosing.
Recommendation

Why A

Waiting research: David Maister's "The Psychology of Waiting Lines" (1985) found that occupied time feels shorter than empty time, and Buell and Norton's "labor illusion" study (Management Science, 2011) found that people value a result more when they can see the work behind it. A keeps the real stage list in view and gives the wait a job. Defaults: Johnson and Goldstein (Science, 2003) showed that most people stay with whatever is pre-selected, so a good pre-pick answers for him most days and a tap is only needed to correct it. HIG: no modal for optional input, 44 pt targets, Dynamic Type to AX sizes. C14: feedback within ~0.1 s, familiar patterns (the occasion question already lives here).

Rules that go with it

The tile shows its check for about 0.4 s, then folds (instant with Reduce Motion). A late answer (after the score) updates the tip lines in place, never the number, and VoiceOver says "Tips updated for minimal." Something else saves his words as onboarding does and guesses no look from them; the advice runs as Not sure. The pre-pick hint always says where it came from ("from this morning's card", "from your last check"), so it never feels like the app decided for him. Mismatch lines name the one piece that pulls away and one move; the "Reads as" line comes back to the card only then.

One call for you

Should the answer ever lower the score when the outfit clearly misses that look (#213)? Recommended: no. The mismatch card already says it plainly, and a guy who says "minimal" in a streetwear fit he likes would lose points for being honest. Default if you don't decide: advice only.

Not shown

The Something else text box (it reuses onboarding's), the re-check card (it inherits the first check's answer and doesn't ask), Gym and Home (no question: there's no look to aim at), and the morning card's own look pill (unchanged, #68–69), which becomes the strongest source of the pre-pick.
