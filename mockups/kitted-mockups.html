Flow 1 · 6 taps to the first check

First run

Onboarding happens inside the chat, with tap answers only, so it teaches the app while it sets it up. Every earlier answer stays on screen as a bubble you can tap to change. Goal, boldness and budget aren't asked yet; they come up later, when they change an answer.

Flow 2 · the core loop

The fit check

Prop the phone, step back, hold still for 3-2-1. The reading screen shows the steps that are really happening, so the wait feels like work getting done. The result is one card: a score with a word, ranked fixes with the predicted gain, and the math behind it one tap away. The AI sees, the code judges, the AI explains.

Flow 3 · why he opens it tomorrow

Every day

The Stylist tab is home. The daily idea arrives as a normal message built from his own closet, at the time he picked. When he's idle, three quick-start chips sit above the composer and the camera becomes a labeled "Check my fit" button, the strongest control on screen. The daily idea is free because it runs on code, not AI.

Flow 4 · look, don't do

Closet, Lookbook, You

These three tabs are for looking. Any button that does something, like "Style this" or "Re-check", jumps to Stylist with the item attached. Progress is quiet and only goes up: Rotation grows with every piece, essentials fill in, and days dressed with intent count up with no chain to break.

Flow 5 · value first, then the ask

Pro and sharing

We only charge for what costs money, which is AI calls. Your score, fixes, closet, Lookbook and outfit ideas are free for good. The paywall shows his own result before the price, and "Continue free" is visible from the first frame. Share cards are never gated and hide the face by default.

What's real and what's not

Sizes are real iOS points: 17 pt body, 44 pt nav bar, 50 pt main buttons, 34 pt chips, 62 pt tab bar. Photos are Unsplash and Pexels test images, never shown with faces. s15 (8.5) and s24 (6.5, partial) carry their real Test 3 scores; the before and after pair is scored by rubric.py from the intended reading. Closet tiles are drawn icons standing in for garment cut-outs.

Checked against

The HIG: labeled floating tab bar, 44 pt tap areas, an X on camera, paywall and sheets, permissions asked in context. The UX checklist in research-context §C14 and spec §2–§13. Every score was run through extraction-test/rubric.py (v3.1).
