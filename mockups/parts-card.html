The parts on the card.
Three ways to show them.

The score becomes a weighted average of five parts: Proportions 30%, Fit 25%, Color 15%, Shoes 15%, Style 15%. Condition only takes points off, and the occasion can only hold the score down. Every option keeps today's card (score and word, summary, fixes, next level) and shows the same two outfits, so only the parts layout changes.

A's fix, checked in the engine

Dark straight jeans alone: Style 6 → 7.5 and Shoes → 8, but the total only goes 6.93 → 7.23, so the card would still say 7.0.

Navy chinos: a complete tailored line (Proportions 8), Style 7.5, Shoes 8 and the bonus: 8.0 Sharp.

So the fix row leads with the chinos, and the jeans become the "smaller step" tip. A fix row never shows a gain that doesn't happen.

Part numbers: whole and half only

Parts show as 7, 7.5, 8.5, never 7.0 or 7.25. The score keeps its one decimal (8.0), so the score always looks like the score and the parts look like what it's made of.

The engine already rounds each part to the half, and the readings are only steady to half a point. A finer number would change between runs of the same photo. The one decimal is the Why sheet's "Average of the parts 7.8".

Option 1 · adds about 95 pt

1A five-part strip under the score

One row of five cells, label above the number, split by hairlines, like the info row at the top of an App Store page. The part the top fix raises gets the copper tint, the same copper as the fix's gain and the ledger's minus values ("points at stake"). One line under it explains the part that matters most.

  • Lead line rule: the lowest part when one is under 7; otherwise the strongest. Fit can't go above 7 today, so "lowest" alone would make every good fit say "Fit 7: nothing looks the wrong size".
  • Condition and occasion lines appear only when they cost something. "Clean" is listed in Why, not on every card.
  • Accessibility sizes: the strip becomes a five-row list (label leading, number trailing). At xxxLarge, still a standard size, it wraps 3 + 2.
  • VoiceOver (one card element): "Parts: Proportions 7, Fit 7, Color 7, Shoes 7.5, Style 6, the lowest. Style 6: the tailored jacket…"
Option 2 · adds about 205 pt

2A meter for each part

Five rows of label, thin ink bar and number, in their own card section. A notch in each bar marks 7, where every part starts. The lowest row carries its reason. Tapping the section opens Why at that part.

  • Clearest picture of the shape of the score, and it stacks well at large sizes.
  • But it's the tallest: the photo bubble scrolls off screen and the fix, the card's one action, moves to the bottom third.
  • Bars read as percentages. A 7 fills 70%, which looks like a C grade when 7 means "fine, nothing off". Five near-identical 7 bars on a plain outfit are noise.
  • Closest to a stat sheet, the gamey look the brand rules out.
Option 3 · adds about 125 pt

3Only the weakest and the strongest

Two rows: "Holding it back" and "Working best", each with the part's number and one reason. The other three parts live in Why. On a check with nothing under 7, the rows are the two strongest.

  • Reads as advice, not a scoreboard, and ties straight to the fix.
  • Easiest at large sizes: it's just text.
  • But it hides three of the five parts, and the parts are the point of the new score. On the many checks where most parts are 7, the second row has little to say.
Recommendation

Option 1, with Option 3's lead line

The strip shows all five parts in a glance and costs less height than a single fix row and its tip. Why Option 1

HIG, layout and typography: put the essential information where it's seen first, support every Dynamic Type size, and stack horizontal content at accessibility sizes. The strip is the most compact way to show all five parts, and it turns into a list at AX sizes. C14, Jakob's Law: it's the App Store's own info row. C14, progressive disclosure: the numbers and one line are on the card; the reasons are one tap away in Why. Brand: no bars or meters, so no stat-sheet look, and copper stays on "points at stake".

Rules that go with it

Lead line: the lowest part if it's under 7, else the strongest, else "Every part is solid: nothing off, nothing standing out yet." Fix rows name their part in the detail ("Raises Style and Proportions"); a part change that doesn't move the shown score becomes a tip, never a fix row. Condition shows only when it costs points ("clean" lives in Why: the AI caught 2 of 19 faults the judges saw, so "clean" on every card would overclaim). Occasion shows only when it holds the score down, with what the parts alone make.

One more call for you

Drop the separate "Reads as …" line from the card. The Style part now carries the look ("Reads as smart casual" is its first reason in Why), and two style lines on one card say the same thing twice. Default if you don't decide: drop it.

Decimals

Parts never get a decimal digit beyond the half (7, 7.5, 8.5). The score always shows one decimal (8.0). The only place a finer number appears is Why's "Average of the parts", one decimal, so the rows can be checked. VoiceOver says "Style 6", never "six point zero".

What's real and what's not

Outfit A, the 5.5, the 7.5 and the 7.0 are real parts-engine v2 outputs (parts_proto.py, test profile Minimal + Smart casual, which never changes a number). Outfit B is composed. Its Style reasons use the old +0.5 standout size (v2 is +0.75). The fix scores for A were run through the engine with the jeans edited. The photos are drawn stand-ins; no real photo is used.
