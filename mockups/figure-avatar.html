The figure, for advice

The founder's call (Oct 14): bring the figure back as an avatar for advice: it wears his own pieces in Closet → Outfits, on the morning card and on his You card. It is a simple drawn figure in the same style as the clothes, with no face, never his photo and never AI. His build is asked politely in onboarding, only to shape advice.

The guardrails that keep it on the right side of "we rate the outfit, never you"
  • Advice only: the figure never appears on a score card, next to a score, or in the Why sheet. Build never changes a score (as today).
  • A drawing, not a body: a plain, faceless, flat figure in one neutral grey (no skin tones, no muscles or abs, no realistic proportions), drawn by code from the same pieces as Closet. Never AI, never a photo of him.
  • Five gentle shapes: Slim · Average · Athletic · Broad · Bigger, plus "Rather not say" (then a neutral average figure). No weight, no measurements, no numbers on the figure.
  • Advice about clothes, never about hiding: "a straight leg balances a roomy top", never "slimming" or "hides your stomach". Changeable any time in You.

Honest notes

  • This reverses part of #84 (Closet: "no avatar"). That research was about AI avatars and try-on, which reviewers say distort bodies and garments. A code-drawn figure wearing his drawn pieces avoids both, so the reasons don't carry over; the photo of outfits he has worn still wins where it exists.
  • The real work is the drawing: each piece needs a "worn" version that fits the figure in 5 builds and 3 fits (about 30 garment types). Roughly 3–4 days on top of the drawn flats, more for layering edge cases (a hoodie under a jacket).
  • Test before shipping: show the build step to 5 guys (it's already on the to-do list as the style-step test) and watch the "Bigger" and "Slim" choices: if anyone hesitates or looks uncomfortable, change the words.
