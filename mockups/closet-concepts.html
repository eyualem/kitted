Kitted · Closet tab research · Oct 8, 2026

What goes in the Closet tab

Three research passes: your own docs and code, 18 competitor apps, and what users say about closet apps. The short version: keep combinations in Closet, show them as a "standing outfit" made of his own pieces instead of an avatar, and make every piece answer "what goes with this?"

The recommendation
  1. Closet gets two views: Pieces and Outfits. Pieces is the grid you already designed. Outfits is the browsable list behind the "fits your closet makes" number, which today is only a number.
  2. No avatar. Show each outfit as his own garments stacked in body order (top, bottoms, shoes) on one tile. It reads like a person without drawing a body.
  3. Every piece answers "what goes with this?" Tapping the grey hoodie shows the 3 best outfits built around it. This is the strongest men-specific hook found.
  4. Outfit ideas come from one engine. The morning card is today's top idea; the Outfits view is the full list; "Goes with" is the same list pinned to one piece. It's all code and free, so no AI cost.
  5. No new tab. Lookbook stays "what I wore" (real photos, real scores). Closet becomes "what I own and what I can make from it".
The screens

How it would look

Wireframes in Kitted's tokens. Garments are drawn icons, as in your mockups, until clean crops exist. The "Outfits" view has no predicted scores, because your spec already rules that out for unworn ideas (02-screens, "no predicted score"). Outfits he has actually worn show their real score.

The avatar question

Don't build one. Use a standing outfit instead.

Your docs already cut avatars and AI try-on (research-context.md; brainstorm 01: "expensive, unreliable, users hate AI art"). The new research makes that case stronger:

It can hurt the user

  • A 2023 Journal of Marketing Research study (6 studies, 8,000+ shoppers) found virtual fitting rooms lowered self-esteem and sales for heavier shoppers. That collides with "never rate bodies".
  • Reviewers say AI avatars make them thinner and taller or add fake abs (TechCrunch on Doji; Khaleej Times, Jan 2026).

It's inaccurate and costly

  • Google's Doppl renders jeans like leg warmers. A long-time Alta user quit the avatar because clothes "change in appearance".
  • Try-on APIs cost about $0.05–0.075 per image, roughly 15× your 0.42¢ scoring call, for every outfit shown.
  • It needs his unblurred face and body sent to another company, which undoes your face-blur design (#54–55).

What to do instead

  • Stack his garments in body order on one tile: top, then bottoms, then shoes, with a layer tucked to the side. Fits (the app) does a version of this with its swipe "Dressing Room".
  • It's free, it's instant, the same outfit always looks the same, and it uses the icons you already draw. Real cut-outs replace the icons later.
  • Outfits he has worn show his own photo, which beats any avatar for realism.
How it gets built

Mostly done already in code

The engine exists. pickOutfit in ideas.ts already tries every valid combination (up to about 600), scores each with the full rubric for the occasion, and keeps the best one. Keeping the top 20 instead of the top 1 gives the Outfits list. Pinning one item, which it already supports for "Style this", gives "Goes with". rotation() already counts combinations; it only needs to return item ids.

It costs nothing to run. No AI call, so it's free on every plan, which matches "everything that runs on code is free".

It fixes an open bug in the plan. The mockup's "37 fits" can't be reproduced. With layers counted, a 16-piece closet makes about 180, which is meaningless (04-logic, open question). Recommended count: top + bottoms + shoes combos the rules rate "Put together" or better; a layer never multiplies the count. It's honest, and it can't be inflated.

Rough size: engine change ½ day, Outfits view 2 days, "Goes with" on the piece screen 1 day. About 3.5 days on top of the Closet tab itself, which the style-council session owns. A swipe shuffle ("pin the chinos, shuffle the rest") is a nice play feature but has no retention evidence, so it waits for v1.1.

Competition

One thing changed in September

A research pass found that Google Photos "Wardrobe" reached all US users on Sep 24, 2026 (TechCrunch). It builds a closet from your photo library automatically and offers mix-and-match plus try-on. I haven't checked it hands-on. If accurate, "the closet fills itself" is no longer unique to Kitted. What's still unique is judgement: the score, the fixes, and outfits ranked by a rubric instead of matched on tags. That's why the Outfits view should be visibly "your stylist's picks", not a random shuffle. Random-looking combos are the top complaint about Cladwell, Whering and Acloset.
